# Authored-choice source review — 29 September 2026

This review accompanies candidate dataset 0.24.6. The registered [Level 2 grape expansion](../../assets/curriculum/templates/wset_l2_authored_choice_grape_expansion.yaml) and [Level 3 New World expansion](../../assets/curriculum/templates/wset_l3_authored_choice_new_world_expansion.yaml) each contain 35 choices about existing facts. Each row names an existing `knowledgeItemId` and that item's `sourceCitationId`; it does not create a new wine fact. The curriculum validator checks that the citation actually belongs to the item, the four options are distinct, and exactly one index is keyed. Static authoring checks found no uniquely longest or shortest option in the Level 2 batch, and no uniquely longest option or uniquely shortest key in the Level 3 batch. These checks do not substitute for qualified wine review.

The Level 2 batch covers Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, Pinot Noir, Sauvignon Blanc, Riesling, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah/Shiraz. The principal-grape session scope follows the [WSET Level 2 session plan](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/13022/wset_l2wines_sessionplans_en_may2023_issue2.pdf). The keyed regional and variety claims were checked against the linked item sources, including [Plantgrape's Chardonnay profile](https://www.plantgrape.fr/fr/varietes/varietes-a-fruits/60), [CIVA's Pinot Gris description](https://www.vinsalsace.com/en/tastes-and-colours/grapes-varieties/powerful-and-intense/pinot-gris-dalsace/), [Wine Australia's Hunter Valley guide](https://www.wineaustralia.com/market-insights/regions-and-varieties/new-south-wales-wines/hunter-valley), [New Zealand Winegrowers' Hawke's Bay guide](https://www.nzwine.com/en/regions/hawkesbay/), [Napa Valley Vintners' grape descriptions](https://wine.napavintners.com/wine), and [Bordeaux's Cabernet Sauvignon](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/grape-varieties/cabernet-sauvignon/) and [Merlot](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/grape-varieties/merlot/) profiles. The key is phrased as the supported example or tendency, not an assertion that every wine or site behaves identically. Inaccessible Champagne, Alsace Riesling and Wine Australia candidate pages were excluded from this batch rather than used without inspection.

The first Level 2 draft had a systematic distractor cue: 27 of 35 uniquely longest options were wrong. A first repair exposed the inverse cue that uniquely shortest options were also always wrong. An independent cross-review prompted revisions to 34 item blocks while preserving all answer indices and exact item-linked citations. The final static pass found four distinct options in all 35 rows and no uniquely longest or shortest option in any row. This removes those measurable cues; it does not establish psychometric quality or rule out every ambiguous distractor.

The item-by-item [Level 2 source audit](wset-l2-grape-expansion-source-audit-2026-09-29.md) and [cross-review](wset-l2-grape-expansion-cross-review-2026-09-29.md) retain exact source locators and scope caveats. The corresponding [Level 3 source audit](wset-l3-new-world-source-audit-2026-09-29.md) and [cross-review](wset-l3-new-world-cross-review-2026-09-29.md) record its 35-item selection and five repairs.

A separate semantic audit joined **all 359 registered choices** to the exact graded assertion, keyed option and item-linked citation. It found six answers that tested an adjacent fact and nine that added an unstudied detail. All 15 were revised around the existing graded assertion; a second pass checked the revised options for unique keys and reasonable parallel alternatives. The Pfalz row now tests the grape range supported by its displayed German Wine Institute regional citation without depending on ungraded planting statistics. The Sauvignon Blanc structure row now cites an opened [Wine Australia technical report](https://www.wineaustralia.com/getmedia/9a026278-cfaa-400c-8bc4-4526ed46c354/WFA-01-01) for the sugar, acidity and potential-alcohol mechanism rather than relying on a regional style page. This is an editorial alignment check, not a qualified factual sign-off.

The Level 3 batch covers 15 Australia, 12 South Africa and 8 Argentina assertions within the broad named-region and growing/production scope of the [WSET Level 3 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf). Representative checks used [Wine Australia's Barossa](https://www.wineaustralia.com/market-insights/regions-and-varieties/south-australia-wines/barossa), [Adelaide Hills](https://www.wineaustralia.com/market-insights/regions-and-varieties/south-australia-wines/adelaide-hills) and [Riverina](https://www.wineaustralia.com/market-insights/regions-and-varieties/new-south-wales-wines/riverina) descriptions, [Wines of Argentina's irrigation presentation](https://api.winesofargentina.org/uploads/2020/01/n5FlX7Bh7x_INTRODUCTION_TO_ARGENTINA_PRESENTATION_-_Eng.pdf), [South Africa's Wine of Origin scheme](https://www.wosa.co.za/The-Industry/Wines-Of-Origin/Wine-Of-Origin-scheme/), and the [Chenin Blanc Association's style examples](https://www.chenin.co.za/styleindicator). Some source sites intermittently failed to load, so all 35 individual claims have not been freshly reverified from opened primary pages in this pass. Their item-linked citations remain visible to learners and their `unverified` status is unchanged.

An independent editorial pass found and repaired five issues before registration: the precise geographical-unit hierarchy in `ki_wset_nw_south_africa_wo_levels`, canopy wording in `ki_reg_sh_swartland_shade`, the potentially competing westward climate trend in `ki_reg_am_patagonia_latitude`, slope/aspect versus drainage in `ki_reg_sh_stellenbosch_aspect`, and the aroma-pair explanation in `ki_reg_sh_chenin_fresh`. The repair makes the intended key narrower and keeps the original citation. No answer is presented as a legal polygon, official WSET exam item, or blind-tasting guarantee.
