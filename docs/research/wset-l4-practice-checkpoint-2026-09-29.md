# Diploma practice checkpoint — 29 September 2026

Release 0.24.58 measures **2,837/2,837 WSET Level 4 core authored items with useful practice** in the generated coverage baseline. The domain counts are geography 1,110/1,110, winemaking 710/710, viticulture 479/479, business 250/250, tasting 178/178, and service 110/110. These are the app's authored-item denominators, not the number of points in the [WSET Level 4 Diploma specification, Issue 1.4](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/17609/wset_l4wines_specification_en_august-2025.pdf). The baseline was regenerated after the business, geography/service, vineyard, winemaking, two D1 method, D4/D5 product and D2 marketing depth batches were combined. Core practice coverage must not be displayed as an official examination result, a WSET qualification, expert-verified accuracy or full syllabus parity.

The practice-closure batch adds item-specific, cited decisions and complete four-role cases for the existing 4,288 assertions. The two D1 method continuations add 39 cited core assertions, the D4/D5 product continuation adds 18, and the D2 marketing depth continuation adds 14; with 5 CMS spirit/liqueur examples, all 4,364 now remain `unverified`. Business case pools also close 33 vineyard and 19 winemaking roles that belonged to cross-domain scenarios. The 24 new D3 geography choices, 51 winemaking choices, 10 sparkling/fortified choices and 6 D2 marketing choices have balanced correct-answer length ranks; their tests check source links, distinct options, plausible distractors and runtime delivery. Learners can also use the existing timed D1/D2 written rehearsal, D6 learner-owned research workspace and physical three-wine D4/D5 tasting flights. Those tools provide practice and recorded self-assessment, not automatic grading to the examining body's standard.

An item-level coverage report finds **581/581 core `LOCATED_IN` facts have spatial practice** in this release; an integrated regression test guards that property. The geography domain also contains regional production, grape, law and case facts. Its 446 core non-spatial items are not missing map coordinates simply because they are classified as geography; a grape-permission map in particular requires a complete, cited legal answer set before other regions can be treated as wrong.

The [specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/17609/wset_l4wines_specification_en_august-2025.pdf) still identifies content and assessment work beyond this numerator:

- D1 now has sourced teaching and practice for flash détente, hyperoxidation, flotation, micro-oxygenation, oak-piece versus barrel choices, layering, mass selection, head grafting, fire/smoke assessment, cryoextraction and pouch/can/glass-stopper comparisons. These additions close the specifically enumerated method omissions; they do not establish exhaustive vineyard/cellar cost evaluation, sustained written reasoning or qualified factual review across D1.
- D3 requires regional style, quality and price analysis and accurate comparative tasting. The atlas has broad named coverage, but three Vino Nobile Pievi, most Soave and Alto Adige UGAs, Paardeberg South and other fine registers still lack defensible distinct markers or full legal geometry. The [fine-map research record](fine-map-roadblocks-2026-09-29.md) documents why four of those places were not guessed. A named map target is not a complete regional lesson.
- D4 and D5 now include cited Crémant, Saumur, Trento, Lambrusco, Sherry and Port decisions, yet still require product-specific analytical tasting and wider regional, legal, production and commercial comparisons. Physical tasting flights have been added, but their evidence is learner-entered and cannot establish sensory accuracy without a qualified reviewer.
- D6 practice supports independent source appraisal and drafting; the live assignment title and official assessment remain external to the app.
