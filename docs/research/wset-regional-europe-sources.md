## Scope

The current WSET Level 2 page links the [2026 Issue 2.1 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf). The current Level 3 page still links [2022 Issue 2](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf). Scope documents identify required subjects, and are never used as wine-law citations. Levels 1–2 supply basic style and grape foundations; advanced regional applications start at Level 3. Grouped Level 2 subset flags in the evidence are proposals, not promotion of every Level 3 named place to Level 2.

Public Level 3 scope requires Tokaj sweet wines; dry Tokaj is contextual. Austria requires Niederösterreich (including Wachau and Weinviertel) and Burgenland; Styria and Vienna remain optional. Greece requires Naoussa, Nemea and Santorini; other Greek atlas depth remains optional. Burgundy and Alsace classification categories are required, not the individual terroir/history of every Grand Cru site.

## Editorial and legal safeguards

- Bordeaux Supérieur includes reds and sweet whites. Saint-Émilion Grand Cru appellation differs from Grand Cru Classé estate status.
- Bourgogne Côte d’Or and the Bourgogne Hautes Côtes/Côte Chalonnaise denominations remain regional Burgundy names; no conversion to Grand Cru.
- Alsace Grand Cru has grape/site exceptions; recent Pinot Noir approvals make a universal four-white-grape-only claim unsafe. VT and SGN differ; VT does not itself prove noble rot.
- Recioto di Soave is a sweet dried-grape white, distinct from Recioto della Valpolicella. Both are explicitly important to current Level 2. Amarone and Recioto differ in fermentation/sugar outcome; Ripasso differs in processing.
- Delle Venezie spans Veneto, Friuli-Venezia Giulia and Trento. Sicilia DOC and Terre Siciliane IGT are wine identities distinct from the administrative island. Vino Nobile di Montepulciano uses Sangiovese; Montepulciano d’Abruzzo names a different grape.
- DO/DOCa/DOQ and Vino de Pago differ from Vino de la Tierra PGI. Valencia and La Mancha protected wine names cannot be replaced by administrative communities. The MAPA register used here was updated 2 July 2026. Rioja generic category need not be unoaked or cheap; ageing label minima are appellation/colour specific, and Toro differs from Rioja. The repealed Spanish 2003 law’s old classification articles are not used.
- German Prädikat concerns ripeness/harvest conditions, not final sweetness; trocken/halbtrocken express regulated taste terms. Private VDP labels remain separate from statutory quality marks and their transition. Austrian Qualitätswein/Prädikatswein/DAC likewise express different information.
- Frascati steel/lees practice and barrel-treated Penedès Xarel·lo are explicitly producer examples, never mandatory regional recipes. Promotional quality rankings, statistics and exact universal sensory guarantees are excluded.

## Integration and review

Only LOCATED_IN items count as location evidence. Production/style/environment dimensions use exact authored principles or case reasoning, never a broad place selector or soil-tag total. Foundation grape profiles, general vine/winery/cost lessons and canonical named nodes can close the remaining flagged dimensions. Root owns these shared additions and source/manifest/release updates; this file owns no geometry or certification claim. MCQ is disabled for conditional profile explanations; generic principle templates provide typed, flashcard and short-answer practice, while existing cases provide structured application. Run the new regression test and full ingestion/validation before release.

Vino de Pago and the VT/SGN comparison start at Level 3. Classico/Riserva and DOC/DOCG/IGT basics begin at Level 2; the more advanced Ripasso process remains at Level 3. Level 2 also names Burgundy Pinot Noir origins, Delle Venezie/Friuli, Puglia, Fiano di Avellino and Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi. These subsets must be retained when integrating the grouped evidence.
