# WSET Level 2–3 business application choices

This release adds four-option practice to 26 **existing** core business facts, without adding, remapping or claiming expert verification for any fact. Fifteen facts are selected at WSET Level 2 and remain available at Level 3; eleven more are selected at Level 3. The prompts are controlled cases, not reports of actual wineries or regional price surveys. In particular, a GI, producer reputation, cooler site, extra oak, sorting or longer holding never guarantees higher sensory quality or a selling price.

The Level 2 IDs are `ki_wset_apply_sauternes_selection_cost` and `ki_wset_nwa_{california,cape_cool,chile_central,chile_coastal,hawkes,marlborough,martinborough,mendoza,napa,oregon,otago,sonoma,stellenbosch,western_cape}_cost`. The eleven Level 3 IDs are `ki_wset_apply_{bordeaux_white,north_italian_white,abruzzo,aragon,duero,northwest,levante,mancha,alentejo,lisboa,rheinhessen}_cost`. The source citation selected for each choice is already attached to its item; the item's other regional citations supply geographic context.

The producer-specific fruit, vineyard condition, vessel and release plans in the questions are explicitly supplied assumptions from the underlying item assertions. Correct answers calculate the specified cost, assign inputs to the responsible lot or stage, or select a route under the stated trial and capacity conditions. Distractors make a distinct error about usable yield, omitted capacity/labour, the stated buyer evidence or an origin-based price. All facts remain `unverified`, and generic MCQ generation remains disabled for these conditional cases.

## Learning-quality revision

The first draft overused one keyed pattern: “cost wood/holding, taste, assess demand.” All 26 questions were revised to require different operations or a more defensible partial-budget comparison. Sauternes, Sonoma and Calatayud now calculate unit cost with the correct **usable-output denominator**; California and Hawke’s Bay compare an actual buyer offer with incremental cost; Willamette includes a displaced vessel use; Central Otago interprets an *expected* avoided loss. Constantia/Elgin and Napa allocate scarce new wood using supplied trial results, Casablanca identifies a tank conflict, Marlborough accounts for replacement fruit, Maipo/Colchagua and Rías Baixas assign costs to the responsible lot or stage, La Mancha chooses harvest timing under a supplied storm deadline, and Alentejo applies a firm budget ceiling. Bordeaux, Soave, Western Cape Chenin and Rheinhessen use stated buyer briefs and sensory trials to choose a route rather than rewarding extra expenditure. The other comparisons distinguish named inputs, usable output and cash timing without converting a region into a price rank.

Every amount, buyer offer, trial result and deadline in these revised cases is **illustrative and explicitly supplied in the prompt**; no cited source claims those particular figures or transactions occurred. Arithmetic answers are conditional, not market forecasts. Several items remain qualitative where another invented number or capacity limit would repeat a mechanism without adding a sourced regional distinction. A qualified reviewer must still assess the teaching level, distractor difficulty and regional fit before promotion from `unverified`.
