Scope, not a fact source. The Court of Master Sommeliers Americas page for the Certified Sommelier Examination, opened 27 September 2026, says the exam is one day and three sections: a written deductive tasting of four wines, a 45-question theory paper covering wine, beverage, and the sommelier trade, and a hospitality section in which a candidate may be asked to open still or sparkling wine, recommend a cocktail or a spirit, and discuss food and wine. A pass is 60 percent on every section in one sitting. That page justifies why beverage identity, allergen declaration, refusal of service, and pairing judgment are in this pack. It is not quoted further. Its grids, syllabus PDF, and service-standards PDF were not copied and are not a source for any fact below.

## What a learner can now practise

1. Tequila identity. Only Agave tequilana Weber blue, only the appellation areas, 35 to 55 percent alcohol. The Council's appellation page gives 181 municipalities in Jalisco, Michoacán, Guanajuato, Nayarit, and Tamaulipas, and a declaration dated 9 December 1974. That is the Council's account, not the gazette. Two categories: 100 percent of the sugars, bottled at origin; or at least 51 percent and up to 49 percent other reducing sugars, which may be bottled outside. Blanco is unaged and adjusted only with water. Joven is a blend. Ageing taught from the maturation paragraph: reposado at least two months, añejo at least one year, extra añejo at least three years, in oak or holm oak. The English class cards on the same page give reposado one year, the same figure they give añejo. The cards are not taught. Their 600-litre figure is not locked. Agave liquors without the tequila rules are not tequila. No cooking temperature and no still procedure.

2. Three grape spirits, compared. Adopted Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2019/787: wine spirit from wine, wine fortified for distillation, or wine distillate, distilled below 86 percent, minimum 37.5 percent. Grape marc spirit from fermented grape marc, also below 86 percent, first distillation in the presence of the marc, lees limits 25 kg per 100 kg and 35 percent of the alcohol, minimum 37.5 percent. The annex does not define grappa. Brandy is wine spirit with an optional wine distillate distilled below 94.8 percent and at most half the alcohol, aged one year in oak of at least 1,000 litres or six months in smaller oak, minimum 36 percent. Cognac, from the BNIC page already in the registered pack, is at least two years on the youngest eau-de-vie and 40 percent at sale. The adopted text is labelled as adopted because the publisher warns that it may have been updated. The current UK annex page blanks categories 1 to 14; that is why the adopted text is the source. The percentages are legal identity, not a distillation method.

3. Beer yeasts, without a founding date. The 2023 FEMS Yeast Research paper (doi 10.1093/femsyr/foad023, opened on PMC) says modern lager is a cold fermentation with Saccharomyces pastorianus, a hybrid of Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Saccharomyces eubayanus, and that ale in the modern sense is a warmer fermentation with S. cerevisiae. It proposes, as a hypothesis, a Munich Hofbräuhaus hybridization in 1602–1615, and it says Bavarian bottom fermentation was older. Medieval slurries are not renamed as the modern hybrid. No fermentation temperature is taught as law. The paper's mention of the 1516 purity ordinance is not a transcription and is not current beer law.

4. Two label sentences. Revised Annex II of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 on legislation.gov.uk: sulphites must be declared above 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre total SO2 in the product as ready to consume. That is not a wine ceiling and not a safety clearance. Fish gelatine or isinglass used as a fining agent in beer and wine is excepted from the fish entry. The exception is not clinical advice.

5. Two service limits. Licensing Act 2003 section 141, England and Wales: do not knowingly sell alcohol to a person who is drunk. The section does not define drunk, is not a world rule, and is not the WHO statement that no level of alcohol is safe. A guest who dislikes a match is offered another wine. No tannin-and-protein page was opened, so none is taught.

Each of those five blocks has a comparison, a misconception, and two applied cases with an action, a reason, a tradeoff, and a limit. Ten question rows carry acceptable phrases, a wrong answer, and why it is wrong. Subjective service rows are `mcq_disabled`.

## Coverage review (this course is not the whole sommelier gap)

Reviewed after the extract file landed. The English tequila class card is not taught. The Spanish Council page, opened separately, agrees with the English maturation paragraph: reposado at least two months. Its añejo and extra añejo cards state 600 litres. Its reposado card does not. The courtesy PDF of NOM-006 and the gazette were not opened, so the further claim that the 49 percent cannot come from any agave stays unresolved.

Added from pages opened in this pass, still unregistered:

- Calvados, from the INAO product note only. Distillate of cider and perry, at least two years in oak. Both a double distillation and a column are named and not described. The note's two commune counts disagree, so neither is taught. No sale strength is stated on that note. The cahier was not opened. Pays d'Auge and Domfrontais ageing are not on this product page.
- Armagnac, from the BNIA distillation page only. About 95 percent continuous copper alambic, 400-litre pièces mostly Gascony or Limousin, 40 percent at consumption, vintage means the harvest year, and bottling stops ageing. The plate-by-plate path and the off-still strength band were on the page and were not copied.

Still unauthored:

- The empty URL `https://www.armagnac.fr/en/discover/armagnac-in-brief/` is not a source. The distillation page above replaced it for identity only.
- Grappa as a geographical indication. The EU category name was opened. The GI register was not.
- The geographical indication grappa. The EU category name was opened. The GI specification was not.
- NOM-006-SCFI-2012 itself, and the 9 December 1974 gazette entry. The Council's summaries are taught as summaries.
- A transcription of the 1516 Reinheitsgebot. It must not be taught as world beer law from the yeast paper.
- Pairing chemistry. The case teaches the refusal to invent it. That is not a pairing textbook.
- Wine-list construction, cellar inventory, and tableside opening steps. The CMS page says a candidate may be asked to open a bottle. The steps live in a standards PDF that this pack does not copy.
- Cigar cut, lighting, and humidity. Harm statements already exist in the registered 0.22.0 pack. No new cigar procedure was added.
- The EU regulation that sets maximum sulphur dioxide in wine. 150 and 200 mg/litre are not taught.
- Ale and lager temperature bands. Deliberately omitted. The paper's approximate figures are not a rule.

Registered 0.22.0 already teaches Scotch, bourbon, rum, gin, vodka, Cognac mentions, cask ale, UK excise cider, sake polishing, Habano anatomy, and the two WHO harm statements. This file does not repeat them as new URLs.
