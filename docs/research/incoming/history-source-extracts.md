## 1. Columella, *De Re Rustica*, Book III

- **URL:** https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Columella/de_Re_Rustica/3*.html
- **Publisher:** LacusCurtius (Bill Thayer), on penelope.uchicago.edu. The page says it reproduces Book III from Volume I of the Loeb Classical Library edition, 1941, and that “The text is in the public domain.”
- **Title:** Columella, *De Re Rustica*, Book III.
- **Date:** The page dates the printed translation to 1941. It does not date Columella or the composition of the work. Do not add a century for the author from this page.

Book III, as opened, gives site choice, matching of vine to soil and climate, training on a tree or a trellis, a home nursery, mallet-shoot cuttings, and grafting to multiply a vine already proved fruitful. It is not a complete training manual. Book IV was not used.

Quotations:

- “Still the wise farmer will have discovered by test that the kind of vine proper for level country is one which endures mists and frosts without injury; for a hillside, one which withstands drought and wind. He will assign to fat and fertile land a vine that is slender and not too productive by nature; to lean land, a prolific vine.”
- “a vineyard does not want an icy climate nor, on the other hand, one that is burning hot, though it thrives better in warm weather than in cold. It is harmed more by rain than by clear weather, and is more kindly disposed to a dry soil than to one that is subject to too much rain.”
- “the best soil, as Celsus very rightly believes, is neither too compact nor loose, but closer to the loose type; neither poor nor excessively rich, but nearest to the fertile kind; neither flat nor steep, but like plain-land with a rise; neither dry nor wet, but moderately moist.”
- “in nearly every type of place champaign slopes produce the larger quantity of wine, but hill lands the better flavoured; and in a temperate climate hills that slope to the north are more productive, while those with a southern exposure yield a superior quality.”
- “We know of two ‘sister’ vines, of which the smaller is earlier and better in casting its blossoms and may be trained to tree and trellis alike. On the tree it requires rich ground; on the trellis, ordinary soil.”
- “he should not buy quicksets. But he should plant at home shoots of the sort most approved, and should make a nursery of vines from which he may clothe his land with vineyards. For foreign cuttings, transplanted from a different locality, are less at home in our soil than are the native varieties.”
- “A vine-nursery should be established in ground that is neither hungry nor wet, but moist and of medium quality rather than fat.”
- “it is the mark of a wise husbandman to transplant from poorer ground to better rather than from better to poorer.”
- “The mallet-shoot, moreover, is a young shoot growing out of a cane of the year before… Our opinion is that this should be taken from the most prolific stock every time that the vines are pruned, and carefully planted, with three or four eyes showing above ground, in soil that is moderately moist but not wet.”
- “when the fruitfulness of the vine has been proved, it is very quickly raised to a very large number by ingrafting.”

The ellipsis in the mallet-shoot quotation skips only the sentence that explains the name (“projecting on both sides… the appearance of a mallet”).

## 2. Eleanor of Aquitaine, 1152

- **URL:** https://www.britannica.com/biography/Eleanor-of-Aquitaine
- **Publisher:** Encyclopaedia Britannica. The page credits Régine Pernoud and says the article was fact-checked by Britannica editors.
- **Title:** “Eleanor of Aquitaine,” described on the page as “queen consort of France and England.”
- **Date:** No publication or revision date was visible on the page that opened. The first fetch of this URL returned only a bot-check page (“Just a moment…”). The article text below is from a second open of the same URL.

The page links the 1152 marriage to Aquitaine and, from 1154, to the English crown. It does not name Bordeaux. It does not mention wine, and it states no trade volume. Do not add either.

Quotations:

- “Eleanor of Aquitaine (born c. 1122—died April 1, 1204, Fontevrault, Anjou, France) was the queen consort of both Louis VII of France (1137–52) and Henry II of England (1152–1204).”
- “Eleanor was the daughter and heiress of William X, duke of Aquitaine and count of Poitiers, who possessed one of the largest domains in France—larger, in fact, than those held by the French king. Upon William’s death in 1137 she inherited the duchy of Aquitaine.”
- “After their return to France and a short-lived reconciliation, their marriage was annulled in March 1152.”
- “According to feudal customs, Eleanor then regained possession of Aquitaine, and two months later she married the grandson of Henry I of England, Henry Plantagenet, count of Anjou and duke of Normandy. In 1154 he became, as Henry II, king of England, with the result that England, Normandy, and the west of France were united under his rule.”

Limitation on the page itself: the consortship date in the first quotation is 1152–1204, while the narrative dates Henry’s accession as king to 1154. The page gives March 1152 for the annulment and only “two months later” for the marriage. It does not give a day in May.

A separate “Why is Eleanor of Aquitaine important?” block on the same page says: “Eleanor was queen consort to Louis VII (1137–52) of France and Henry II of England (1152–1204).”

## 3. Italian denominazione di origine, DPR 12 July 1963, n. 930

- **URL (text read):** https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1963/07/15/188/so/1/sg/pdf
- **Act landing page, which does not itself print the articles:** https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/serie_generale/caricaDettaglioAtto/originario?atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=1963-07-15&atto.codiceRedazionale=063U0930
- **Publisher:** *Gazzetta Ufficiale della Repubblica Italiana*. The supplement masthead reads “Supplemento ordinario alla ‘Gazzetta Ufficiale’, n. 188 del 15 luglio 1963” and “Roma - Lunedì, 15 luglio 1963.” The PDF is served by gazzettaufficiale.it. Page 2 of the downloaded scan was blank; the decree text begins on the page numbered 3.
- **Title:** “DECRETO DEL PRESIDENTE DELLA REPUBBLICA 12 luglio 1963, n. 930. Norme per la tutela delle denominazioni di origine dei mosti e dei vini.”
- **Date:** Decree dated 12 July 1963; published in the ordinary supplement to GU n. 188 of 15 July 1963.

Normattiva’s original-text view was also opened: https://www.normattiva.it/atto/caricaDettaglioAtto?atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=1963-07-15&atto.codiceRedazionale=063U0930&tipoDettaglio=originario

That view printed Article 1 and labelled it “Testo in vigore dal: 30-7-1963 al: 10-5-2010.” Its Article 1 matches the Gazzetta wording below. The full-act export on Normattiva (`/esporta/attoCompleto` for the same act) returned only an error page, so Articles 2 and 4 are quoted from the Gazzetta scan, not from that export. A later consolidated copy on edizionieuropee.it was opened and then set aside: it marks Articles 4 and 6 as later repealed, so it is not the 1963 print.

The 1963 text creates three grades. It does not use the later abbreviations DOC and DOCG.

Quotations from the Gazzetta print:

- Article 1: “Per denominazioni di origine dei vini s’intendono i nomi geografici e le qualificazioni geografiche delle corrispondenti zone di produzione — accompagnati o non con nomi di vitigni o altre indicazioni — usati per designare i vini che ne sono originari e le cui caratteristiche dipendono essenzialmente dai vitigni e dalle condizioni naturali di ambiente.”
- Article 1, second paragraph: “La zona di produzione di cui al precedente comma può comprendere, oltre il territorio indicato nella rispettiva denominazione di origine, anche i territori vicini, quando in essi esistono analoghe condizioni naturali e, alla data di entrata in vigore del presente decreto, si producono, da almeno dieci anni, vini immessi sul mercato con la medesima denominazione, purché abbiano analoghe caratteristiche chimico-fisiche ed organolettiche e siano prodotti con uve provenienti dai vitigni tradizionali della zona, vinificate con i metodi di uso generalizzato della zona stessa.”
- Article 2: “Le denominazioni di origine dei vini sono distinte in: a) denominazioni di origine « semplice »; b) denominazioni di origine « controllata »; c) denominazioni di origine « controllata e garantita ».”
- Article 4: “Le denominazioni di origine « controllate » sono riservate a vini che rispondono alle condizioni ed ai requisiti stabiliti, per ciascuna di esse, nei relativi disciplinari di produzione.”
- Article 4 continues: “Le denominazioni di origine « controllate e garantite » sono riservate a vini di particolare pregio — da immettersi al consumo con le modalità previste dal successivo articolo 7 — che rispondono alle condizioni ed ai requisiti stabiliti, per ciascuna di esse, nei relativi disciplinari di produzione.”
- Article 4, later on the same page: “L’uso delle denominazioni di origine « controllate » e « controllate e garantite » non è consentito per i vini ottenuti sia totalmente che parzialmente da vitigni ibridi produttori diretti.”

The Normattiva original-text view, for the article it displayed, limits that text to 30 July 1963–10 May 2010. This extract does not open the later repealing act.

### 4a. INRAE aphid encyclopaedia

- **URL:** https://eng-encyclopedie-pucerons.hub.inrae.fr/species/aphids/daktulosphaira-phylloxera/daktulosphaira-vitifoliae-fitch-1851
- **Publisher:** INRAE, *Encyclopédie des pucerons* (English page). The page says “By: F. Duval, C. Buchard, J-C. Simon.”
- **Title:** “Daktulosphaira vitifoliae (Fitch 1851),” with the English name “Grapevine Phylloxera.”
- **Date on the page:** “Modification date: 22 May 2024 | Publication date: 27 April 2021.”

Quotations:

- “The end of the 19th century was marked by a serious wine health crisis for French producers, caused by the importation from the USA of *Phylloxera* (now *Daktulosphaira vitifoliae*). Between 1863 and 1893, *D. vitifoliae* destroyed a very large part of the French vineyard and caused the disappearance of European grape varieties and vineyards.”
- “It seems that the importation of *Vitis riparia*, a wild American vine, was responsible for the introduction of *Phylloxera* into Europe.”
- “One solution used to limit the spread of aphids in crops is to graft *Vitis vinifera* onto resistant rootstocks of American origin. Another solution is to grow the vines in sandy soil, which prevents the neogallicolous-radicolous individuals from descending to the roots to form new galls.”

Limitation on the page itself: the introduction route is hedged (“It seems that”). Grafting is “one solution,” and sandy soil is named as another. The same sentence that says “a very large part of the French vineyard” also says “the disappearance of European grape varieties and vineyards,” which is wider than France and is not given a further limit on this page. Do not treat that second clause as a measured census.

### 4b. INRAE press release

- **URL:** https://www.inrae.fr/sites/default/files/pdf/CP_phylloxera_VF.pdf
- **Publisher:** INRAE (Institut national de recherche pour l’agriculture, l’alimentation et l’environnement).
- **Title:** “Phylloxéra : la génomique éclaire l’histoire de l’invasion du vignoble français et révèle une nouvelle famille de gènes.”
- **Date:** “Communiqué de presse – 23 juillet 2020.” The release says the underlying paper was published the same day in *BMC Biology* (DOI on the release: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-020-00820-5). That journal article was not opened.

Quotations:

- “Le phylloxéra de la vigne, insecte cousin des pucerons, a dévasté le vignoble français au 19ème siècle suite à une introduction accidentelle. Le risque grave qu’il représentait est aujourd’hui maîtrisé grâce à des porte-greffes tolérants, mais l’insecte est toujours présent et peu connu.”
- “La crise phylloxérique conduisit à la destruction de la moitié du vignoble français modifiant pour longtemps l’économie et la géographie de la viticulture. C’est finalement l’utilisation de vignes américaines résistantes comme porte-greffes qui sauvera la viticulture et permettra la renaissance du vignoble européen.”
- “Ils confirment l’identification de Vitis riparia, une vigne sauvage américaine, comme source des phylloxéras qui ont envahi l’Europe. Plus précisément, les populations sauvages localisées le long du fleuve Mississipi seraient à l’origine de l’introduction du phylloxéra dans le vignoble français.”

This release dates the crisis only as the 19th century. It does not give 1863. “seraient à l’origine” is the release’s own hedge on the Mississippi source. “la moitié du vignoble français” is this page’s figure, and it is not the same claim as the encyclopaedia’s “disappearance of European grape varieties.”

## 5. Clos de Vougeot and the monks of Cîteaux

- **URLs opened:** https://www.closdevougeot.fr/en/ and https://www.closdevougeot.fr/en/history
- **Publisher:** Château du Clos de Vougeot (the site presents itself as the château and as “Headquarters of the Climats de Bourgogne”).
- **Titles:** “The Château du Clos de Vougeot” and “Nine centuries of History.”
- **Date:** No publication date is printed on either page. The history page uses the year-headings quoted below.

The two pages on the same site do not give one founding year. The homepage states the 12th century. The history page puts the Vougeot buildings under a heading “1098,” which is the 11th century. Both are quoted. Neither page was smoothed into a single date.

A press-PDF URL on the same host (`/f/revue_presse/en/25/id1/discover-our-tourism-offers-of-2023.pdf`) returned no text and is not quoted.

Quotations from the homepage:

- “Standing in the very heart of Burgundy’s vineyards, it was originally a wine farm, built in the 12th century by monks from the nearby Abbey of Cîteaux. In the 16th century, a Renaissance style château was added to the existing buildings.”
- “With its medieval vat-house and presses, Cistercian cellar and original kitchens, it forms a unique architectural whole.”
- “The vineyard extends over almost fifty-one uninterrupted hectares.”
- “The Clos de Vougeot did not appear overnight, or even over a century.”

Quotations from the history page:

- Under the heading “1098”: “In those far off days, the monks from Cîteaux cultivated their vines with an intuitive understanding of the different terroirs and their infinite complexity, identifying Burgundy’s Climats nine centuries before they were officially - and universally - recognized. These seasoned vignerons, land-clearers as much as builders, decided to establish at Vougeot the buildings needed to exploit their vast vine lands: the cuverie, or vat house… and the Grand Cellier.”
- Under the heading “1551”: “In 1551, Dom Loisier, forty-eighth abbot of Cîteaux, planted among the already renowned vines a Renaissance manor house.”

The history page’s “1098” section does not itself say “in 1098 the clos was founded.” It attaches that heading to the monks of Cîteaux and to their decision to build at Vougeot. The homepage’s century for the wine farm is the 12th.

## 6. Australian geographical indications (statute)

One New World institutional fact. The Judgment of Paris and the first US AVA rule were not reopened.

- **URL of the compilation that was read:** https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02362/2025-02-21/2025-02-21/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html
- **Register page for the same compilation:** https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02362/2025-02-21/2025-02-21/text
- **Publisher:** Federal Register of Legislation. The register page says the Act is administered by the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry.
- **Title:** *Wine Australia Act 2013*, “No. 161, 1980,” Compilation No. 42.
- **Date:** “Compilation date: 21 February 2025.” The compilation note says this is “the text of the law as amended and in force on 21 February 2025” and “Includes amendments: Act No. 14, 2025.”

The current wording and the 1993 amending act are both on this compilation. Endnote 2 defines “ad = added or inserted.” Endnote 4 records “Part VIB … ad No 93, 1993” and “s 40N … ad No 93, 1993.” Endnote 3’s columns are Act, Number and year, Assent, Commencement, and Application, saving and transitional provisions. In that table the row is: Australian Wine and Brandy Corporation Amendment Act 1993; 93, 1993; assent 16 Dec 1993; commencement 16 Dec 1993; application column “s 15(2).”

Section 4, where the definition now sits, is recorded in Endnote 4 as amended by several Acts, including No. 93, 1993. The definition quoted below is the wording in force on 21 February 2025, not a claim that every word of it was enacted in 1993.

Quotations:

- “geographical indication, in relation to wine goods, means an indication that identifies the goods as originating in a country, or in a region or locality in that country, where a given quality, reputation or other characteristic of the goods is essentially attributable to their geographical origin.”
- Section 40A: “The object of this Part is to regulate the sale, export and import of wine: (a) for the purpose of enabling Australia to fulfil its obligations under prescribed wine-trading agreements and other international agreements; and (b) for certain other purposes for which the Parliament has power to make laws.”
- Section 40N: “A committee to be known as the Geographical Indications Committee is established.”
- Section 40P: “The functions of the Committee are: (a) to deal with applications for the determination of geographical indications for wine in relation to regions and localities in Australia (Australian GIs) in accordance with this Part; and (b) to make determinations of Australian GIs in accordance with this Part (including determining any conditions of use applicable to such GIs).”
- Section 40PA: “This Division deals with the determining of geographical indications (GIs) in relation to a region or locality in Australia.”

The dated institutional fact this compilation will support, and no wider one: section 40N, which establishes the Geographical Indications Committee, and Part VIB were added by Act No. 93 of 1993, the Australian Wine and Brandy Corporation Amendment Act 1993, which this compilation records as assented to and commenced on 16 December 1993.
