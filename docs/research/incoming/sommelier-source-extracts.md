# Sommelier source extracts

Opened 27 September 2026. Research extracts only. This file does not author curriculum, does not change `curriculum.yaml`, and does not claim expert verification or a qualification. Quotations are short and limited to the narrow claim under each heading. Pages that did not return usable text are marked UNRESOLVED. No percentage, temperature, or humidity figure is used unless the opened page states it.

Court of Master Sommeliers material below is examination scope from the Americas court's public pages. It is not a source of wine-law facts. Sample questions, matching items, map prompts, and tasting-grid wording in the exam guide were not copied.

No Reinheitsgebot claim is made. A transcription of the 1516 Bavarian ordinance was not opened.

## 1. Court of Master Sommeliers, Americas: Introductory and Certified scope

Publisher: The Court of Master Sommeliers, Americas.

### Introductory Sommelier Course and Examination

- URL: https://www.mastersommeliers.org/certification/certification-levels/introductory-sommelier-course-and-exam/
- Title: Introductory Sommelier Course & Exam

Scope list, quoted from that page:

- The in-person course covers "a lot of information (and by no means all) about the world of wine and other beverages, as well as several tasting exercises, and wine service demonstrations."
- Preparation named on the page: "the major wine producing countries and regions" and "the basic points of beer and spirits production."
- What the page says a successful candidate has received: "an overview of all the major wine growing regions of the world, as well as key information on spirits, beer, saké and correct wine service."
- The same list also says the candidate has learned "the Court of Master Sommeliers Deductive Tasting Method (DTM)."

Narrow claim: the public Introductory page examines, at overview depth, major wine regions, spirits, beer, saké, wine service, and the Court's deductive tasting method. It does not publish a finer beverage syllabus on this page.

### Certified Sommelier Examination

- URL: https://www.mastersommeliers.org/certification/certification-levels/certified-sommelier-examination/
- Title: Certified Sommelier Examination
- Syllabus PDF linked from that page: https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Certified-Sommelier-Exam-Syllabus.pdf
- Title on the PDF: Certified Sommelier Exam Syllabus. Footer: "© Court of Master Sommeliers, Americas 7.2023".
- Exam-guide PDF linked from that page: https://www.mastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Certified-Sommelier-Exam-Guide-What-to-Expect.pdf
- Title on the PDF: Certified Sommelier Exam — Exam Guide: What to Expect & How to Prepare for the Certified Sommelier Exam.

Scope list. The examination page and both PDFs describe one day and three sections:

1. Deductive tasting.
2. Theory, also called on the PDFs "Theory / Business of the Sommelier".
3. Hospitality and service practical.

The examination page's own section descriptions:

- Tasting: describe and identify four wines, two white and two red, using the Court's deductive tasting method. Grid artwork and grape lists are not copied here.
- Theory: "knowledge and understanding of the world of wine, beverage, and the sommelier trade."
- Hospitality and service: "open still or sparkling wines, to recommend cocktails or spirits, to discuss food and wine pairing options, and to generally show their abilities as a restaurant sommelier."

The syllabus PDF's "Expected skills set of a Certified Sommelier" is the published skill list:

- "Working theoretical knowledge of principal beverage alcohol."
- Tableside service, including "Still and sparkling wine bottle and glass pour service"; "Food and wine pairing salesmanship"; "Cocktail, beer, aperitif, digestif, and fortified wine knowledge"; "Benchmark producer and vintage basics for iconic wine regions."
- "Deductive Tasting Method ability for core classic red and white grape varieties."
- "Basic financials and wine program management."

The exam guide repeats that skill list and, in its hospitality section, says candidates may be asked to "open still or sparkling wines, recommend cocktails, spirits, or beer, discuss food and wine paring options." The spelling "paring" is the guide's. The HTML examination page's service sentence does not name beer; the syllabus skill list and the exam guide do.

Narrow claim: public CMS-Americas Certified scope is deductive tasting of classic red and white wines; theory of wine and other beverages, including cocktail, beer, apéritif, digestif, and fortified wine; and tableside hospitality and service (still and sparkling wine, food-and-wine salesmanship, and those other beverages), plus basic beverage-program financial skill. This note does not treat that list as the Court of Master Sommeliers Europe syllabus. CMS Europe was not opened.

Not copied: the guide's sample theory questions, matching exercises, business-arithmetic examples, cocktail-name lists, producer prompts, and filled tasting grids.

## 2. Tequila: blue agave, authorized territory, 100 percent agave and the other category

Publisher: Consejo Regulador del Tequila (CRT), Tequila Regulatory Council.

- Spanish product page, title "Nuestro Tequila": https://www.crt.org.mx/nuestro-tequila/
- English product page, title "Our Tequila" / "About Tequila": https://www.crt.org.mx/en/our-tequila/
- English appellation page, title "Tequila Appellation of Origin": https://www.crt.org.mx/en/appellation-of-origin/
- Courtesy English translation of the standard, hosted by CRT: https://www.crt.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NOM-006-SCFI-2012%20-%20INGLES.pdf
- Title on that PDF: Official Mexican Standard NOM-006-SCFI-2012, Alcoholic Beverages — Tequila — Specifications. The PDF is labeled "COURTESY TRANSLATION". The issuing authority named in the translation is the Ministry of the Economy (Secretaría de Economía).

UNRESOLVED: the Diario Oficial HTML for NOM-006-SCFI-2012 (`dof.gob.mx` normas page) did not load. The category rule below is therefore taken from the CRT Spanish page and from CRT's courtesy translation, not from the gazette text itself.

### Blue agave

CRT Spanish page: "El Tequila es una bebida alcohólica regional que sólo se obtiene del Agave *tequilana* Weber variedad azul". The same page's requirements: the drink must have been "elaborada a partir del Agave *tequilana* Weber variedad azul" and produced "en las zonas autorizadas por la Denominación de Origen".

Courtesy translation, section 4.2: "The only species allowed for purposes of this NOM, is the tequilana weber blue variety, grown and harvested within the territory specified in the Declaration."

Appellation page: "Only the blue agave (Agave tequilana Weber) is permitted for Tequila production."

### Authorized territory

The courtesy translation does not, in the sections read, list the municipalities. It refers to "the federal states and municipalities indicated in the Declaration" and to "the territory specified in the Declaration."

The CRT appellation page states the present area: "This area encompasses 181 municipalities across five states: Jalisco, Michoacán, Guanajuato, Nayarit, and Tamaulipas." It also says the General Declaration of Protection was published in the Official Gazette of the Federation on 9 December 1974. This note does not treat that 1974 text as an unchanged municipality list, and it does not say that every municipality of those five states is included. The page's soil and climate figures are not used.

### Two categories, and the word "mixto"

The opened CRT pages and the courtesy translation do not use the word "mixto". The legal category names they use are "100% de agave" (and the other legends quoted below) and "Tequila".

CRT Spanish page:

- "Tequila 100% Agave": "Elaborado al 100% con los azúcares del *Agave tequilana Weber variedad azul* y envasado de origen."
- "Tequila": "Elaborado al menos en un 51% con los azúcares del Agave tequilana Weber variedad azul y susceptible a ser enriquecido con un máximo del 49% de otros azúcares reductores. Puede ser envasado dentro y fuera del territorio designado por la DOT."

The English CRT page states the same split: "Made from 100% of the sugars of the Agave *tequilana Weber blue* variety and bottled at origin" and "Made with at least 51% of sugars tequilana Weber blue variety and can be enhanced with a maximum of 49% of other reducing sugars. It can be bottled inside and outside the territory defined by the Appellation of Origin."

Courtesy translation, section 5.1.1 "100% agave": fermentation "may not be enhanced with sugars other than those obtained from the tequilana weber blue variety Agave grown in the territory specified in the Declaration", and the product "must be bottled in the bottling plant controlled by the Authorized Producer, which must be located within the territory specified in the Declaration." Legends quoted there: "100% de agave", "100% puro de agave", "100% agave", or "100% puro agave", to which "azul" may be added.

Courtesy translation, section 6.3, for the category in section 5.1.2: the product "may be enhanced with other sugars in the fermentation process up to a proportion not to exceed 49% of total reducing sugars expressed in units of mass"; "cold mixing is not permitted"; that 49 percent "may not be done with sugars from any species of Agave"; "The 51% of total reducing sugars expressed in units of mass may only be enhanced with tequilana weber blue variety Agave grown in the territory specified in the Declaration." The English of section 5.1.2 in the same PDF is awkward. The Spanish CRT sentence and section 6.3 are the wording relied on. The 51 percent and 49 percent figures are those pages' figures, not a guess.

Do not use the English CRT "classes" blurbs for ageing. On the English product page opened here, the Reposado and Añejo blurbs both say a minimum of one year, while the maturation paragraph on that same English page, the Spanish page, and the courtesy translation say reposado at least two months, añejo at least one year, and extra añejo at least three years. Class ages are not part of the claim above.

The CRT "must" list also states an alcoholic strength "entre 35% Alc. Vol. y 55% Alc. Vol." That figure is quoted only as part of that list. Cooking temperatures and distillation steps on the CRT production section are not extracted.

## 3. Armagnac: still type and region, only as armagnac.fr states them

Publisher: Bureau National Interprofessionnel de l'Armagnac (BNIA), site armagnac.fr. The pages carry the BNIA site and a Ministry of Agriculture mark. These are BNIA pages, not the Cognac interprofession and not the INAO specification.

- Title "Distillation and ageing": https://www.armagnac.fr/distillation-and-ageing
- Title "Grape varieties and Terroirs": https://www.armagnac.fr/grape-varieties-and-terroirs
- Title "FAQ": https://www.armagnac.fr/faq
- Title "The separated brothers: A taste of savoir-faire (3/3)", published on the site 13 October 2015: https://www.armagnac.fr/en/magazine/the-separated-brothers-a-taste-of-savoir-faire-3-3/383/0/4

### Region, from the terroir page

"Bas Armagnac, Armagnac Tenarèze and Haut Armagnac together constitute a vineyard in the form of a vine leaf ... over a part of the three departments: The Gers, the Landes and the Lot-et-Garonne."

That page also says the vineyard "represents 20 000 hectares of vines planted (shared with the IGP Côtes de Gascogne and PDO Floc de Gascogne, of which today, 5200 hectares are identified exclusively for the production of Armagnac)". Those hectare figures are that page's figures only. They were not checked against the cahier des charges. The terroir page does not mention Cognac.

The same page calls Armagnac "a wine eau-de-vie" and an AOC, and says that, unlike appellation wine, "Armagnac has not passed into PDO ... as it does not apply to spirits at the moment."

### Still type

Distillation page: "Most of Armagnac (about 95%) is obtained using a specific alambic for this eau-de-vie: a continuous Armagnac alambic. It is a pure copper apparatus..." The column-by-column description on that page is not copied. The "about 95%" is the page's figure.

FAQ, "Why do certain producers use the double distillation alambic?": "Certain Armagnac houses have chosen to produce a part of their production with a double distillation alambic (authorized since 1972 subject to also having a traditional Armagnac alambic), often used for eaux-de-vie to be marketed whilst very young."

So the opened BNIA pages do not say that every Armagnac is distilled only in a continuous still.

### What the BNIA page says about Cognac

The 2015 magazine article, not the distillation page, draws the contrast: "In the Cognac region, there is only one way to make an eau-de-vie: the distillation must take place in a double distillation pot still." Armagnac's own technique "is mostly used" and "is distilled in an Armagnac alambic, known as continuous distillation". The article also says the wood choice diverged: "Limousin oak from the Charente producers and Gascon oak for the most part, for the Armagnac producers."

Limit: that Cognac sentence is BNIA's 2015 comparison. The Cognac decree and the BNIC site were not opened, so this note does not treat "only one way" as a quotation from Cognac law. The distillation page, separately, says Armagnac is aged in 400-litre oak barrels "mostly from the forests of Gascony or Limousin." Do not teach that Armagnac oak is only Gascon.

The plate-by-plate operation of the continuous still, off-still strengths, and ageing practice on the distillation page are not extracted. No distillation method is taught here.

## 4. Calvados: eau-de-vie of cider fruit, in a delimited appellation, not the cider itself

Publisher: Institut national de l'origine et de la qualité (INAO).

- URL: https://www.inao.gouv.fr/produit/calvados-17614
- Title: Calvados
- Product family printed on the page: "Eaux-de-vie de cidre et de poiré"

Product paragraph: "Le Calvados est une eau-de-vie élaborée à partir de pommes à cidre et de poires à poiré. Les eaux-de-vie obtenues par distillation des cidres et poirés sont vieillies en fûts de chêne pendant au moins 2 ans."

Narrow claim: INAO presents Calvados as an eau-de-vie made from cider apples and perry pears, obtained by distilling cider and perry, then aged at least two years in oak. It is classified with eaux-de-vie de cidre et de poiré. The page does not contain the sentence "Calvados is not cider." The distinction supported here is categorical: Calvados is the distillate of cider and perry, not the fermented cider or perry itself. It is not apples alone; perry pears are in the same sentence.

The page presents a delimited appellation area and warns: "Seul le cahier des charges fait foi" and "Seuls les textes publiés au JO ont une valeur juridique."

The same page gives two area descriptions that do not match. The narrative says a large part of former Basse-Normandie (Manche, Calvados, and Orne), plus part of Eure, Seine-Maritime, Mayenne, Sarthe, and Oise, "environ 1600 communes." The technical block says 1,566 communes wholly and 228 in part, and adds Eure-et-Loir to the department list. No commune count is adopted. The cahier des charges itself was not opened.

The page's sentence on double distillation or a continuous column still is not used. It is not needed for the claim, and it is not a method.

## 5. EU spirit categories: wine spirit and grape marc

Publisher: European Union, Official Journal.

- URL of the text opened (EUR-Lex PDF of the act as published): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32019R0787
- Title: Regulation (EU) 2019/787 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 April 2019 on the definition, description, presentation and labelling of spirit drinks, the use of the names of spirit drinks in the presentation and labelling of other foodstuffs, the protection of geographical indications for spirit drinks, the use of ethyl alcohol and distillates of agricultural origin in alcoholic beverages, and repealing Regulation (EC) No 110/2008
- Official Journal: L 130, 17 May 2019. The HTML EUR-Lex view did not return body text; the PDF did. This is the act as adopted, not a later consolidation.

Annex I, category 4, "Wine spirit". Supporting clauses:

- "(i) it is produced exclusively by the distillation at less than 86 % vol. of wine, wine fortified for distillation or wine distillate distilled at less than 86 % vol."
- "(b) The minimum alcoholic strength by volume of wine spirit shall be 37,5 %."
- "(c) No addition of alcohol, diluted or not, shall take place."
- "(d) Wine spirit shall not be flavoured. This shall not preclude traditional production methods."

Annex I, category 6, "Grape marc spirit or grape marc". Supporting clauses:

- "(i) it is produced exclusively from grape marc fermented and distilled either directly by water vapour or after water has been added", and "each and every distillation is carried out at less than 86 % vol." and "the first distillation is carried out in the presence of the marc itself".
- "(b) The minimum alcoholic strength by volume of grape marc spirit or grape marc shall be 37,5 %."
- "(c) No addition of alcohol, diluted or not, shall take place."
- "(d) Grape marc spirit or grape marc shall not be flavoured. This shall not preclude traditional production methods."

The legal names in the annex are "wine spirit" and "grape marc spirit or grape marc". The word "Grappa" does not occur in the PDF that was opened.

UNRESOLVED: an eAmbrosia register page for the geographical indication Grappa did not return an entry. This note does not treat Grappa as a synonym written into Annex I. Category 6 is the grape-marc category. Whether a registered name "Grappa" sits in that category is not established from the pages that opened.

The same category points also set volatile-substance minima, methanol maxima, a lees limit for grape marc, caramel and sweetening limits, and, for wine spirit, a maturation cross-reference to brandy. Those clauses were read and are not transcribed here, so they are not to be replaced by memory. Nothing in this section is a distillation method.

## 6. Beer yeast: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and *Saccharomyces pastorianus*

Publisher: Versuchs- und Lehranstalt für Brauerei in Berlin (VLB) e.V., Forschungsinstitut für Bier- und Getränketechnologie / Forschungsinstitut für Mikrobiologie.

Two final reports were opened. MDPI, PubMed Central, and the Oxford Academic HTML for a Weihenstephan paper did not return article text (access denied or a browser check). The VLB reports are the sources used.

### Ale and lager names

- URL: https://www.vlb-berlin.org/sites/default/files/2020-04/VLB_16713N_High_Gravity_Fermentationen_2015.pdf
- Title: Schlussbericht. Forschungsthema: "Einbringung von aktuell nicht in der Brauerei eingesetzten Hefen zur Optimierung von High-Gravity-Brewing". AiF 16713 N. Project period 1 October 2012 to 31 March 2015. Contact named on the report: Dr.-Ing. Roland Pahl.

Quotations, hyphenation restored only where the PDF broke a word at a line ending:

- "Die untergärige Bierhefe, Saccharomyces pastorianus, ... Sie wird vor allem für die Lagerbierproduktion verwendet."
- "Die obergärige Brauhefe Saccharomyces cerevisiae ... Diese Brauhefeart wird vor allem für die Aleproduktion verwendet."

Narrow claim: this VLB report assigns *Saccharomyces pastorianus* to bottom-fermenting yeast used above all for lager, and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* to top-fermenting yeast used above all for ale.

Not adopted from that paragraph: its statement that *S. pastorianus* is a hybrid with a *Saccharomyces bayanus*-type parent, its "90%" market sentence, and the fermentation-temperature bands it cites to Lodolo et al. 2008. Those cited works were not opened. The temperature and market figures are therefore not reused.

### Species names without the ale/lager labels

- URL: https://www.vlb-berlin.org/sites/default/files/2020-06/VLB_VF150039_Optimierung_oberg%C3%A4riger_Hefest%C3%A4mme_2018.pdf
- Title: Schlussbericht. Projekttitel: "Optimierung obergäriger Hefestämme". Förderkennzeichen 49VF150039. Research centre: Forschungsinstitut für Bier- und Getränkeproduktion (FIBGP) at VLB. Project period 1 May 2016 to 30 April 2018. Contact named: Dr.-Ing. Katrin Schreiber.

Quotation: "Bei den obergärigen Hefen handelt es sich um Saccharomyces cerevisiae. ... während die untergärigen Hefen (Saccharomyces pastorianus) eine höhere Distanz zu den obergärigen Hefen aufweisen."

That sentence supports the species assignment for top-fermenting and bottom-fermenting brewing yeasts in the strains that report fingerprinted. It does not itself use the words ale or lager. Read it with the 2015 report for those beverage names.

## 7. EU food information: sulphur dioxide above the Annex II threshold

Publisher: European Union, Official Journal.

- URL of the text opened (EUR-Lex PDF of the act as published): https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32011R1169
- Title: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2011 on the provision of food information to consumers
- Official Journal: L 304, 22 November 2011. This extract is the act as published in 2011, not a later consolidation.

Annex II, point 12, states the threshold. It does not name wine:

"Sulphur dioxide and sulphites at concentrations of more than 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre in terms of the total SO2 which are to be calculated for products as proposed ready for consumption or as reconstituted according to the instructions of the manufacturers."

The threshold quoted is only that one: more than 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre, as total SO2. The annex does not say "10 or above".

How the same regulation reaches a beverage such as wine, without a wine-only sentence in point 12:

- Article 9(1)(c) makes mandatory the indication of "any ingredient or processing aid listed in Annex II or derived from a substance or product listed in Annex II causing allergies or intolerances used in the manufacture or preparation of a food and still present in the finished product, even if in an altered form."
- Article 16(4): the ingredient list and the nutrition declaration, points (b) and (l) of Article 9(1), "shall not be mandatory for beverages containing more than 1,2 % by volume of alcohol." Point (c), the Annex II particular, is not in that exemption.
- Article 21: "In the absence of a list of ingredients, the indication of the particulars referred to in point (c) of Article 9(1) shall comprise the word 'contains' followed by the name of the substance or product as listed in Annex II."

Narrow claim: under the 2011 text, sulphur dioxide and sulphites are an Annex II substance once the concentration is more than 10 mg/kg or 10 mg/litre of total SO2. A beverage over 1.2% alcohol by volume is exempt from the ingredient list, not from that Annex II particular, and without an ingredient list the form given in Article 21 is "contains" plus the Annex II name. Wine is not named in Annex II point 12. Applying the rule to wine depends on wine being a food and a beverage above 1.2% alcohol by volume. Oenological maximum sulphur-dioxide limits in other wine regulations were not opened and are not this labelling threshold.

Annex II point 4's exception for fish gelatine or isinglass used as a fining agent in beer and wine was read. It is not a sulphur-dioxide rule and is not used here.

## 8. Refusing further alcohol service

Publisher: The Community Guide, reporting a finding of the Community Preventive Services Task Force. The finding statement is also filed in CDC Stacks, as linked from the page.

- URL: https://www.thecommunityguide.org/findings/alcohol-excessive-consumption-responsible-beverage-service-training.html
- Title: Alcohol Excessive Consumption: Responsible Beverage Service Training
- Finding date printed on the page: October 2010

One principle, from the "Intervention" section. Server training "may address" several practices, including: "Identifying intoxicated patrons and refusing service to them."

The same section says the programs "provide information and teach participants how to serve alcohol responsibly and fulfill the legal requirements of alcohol service." This note does not adopt any jurisdiction's offence, penalty, or police procedure. None of that is quoted.

Limit: the task force "finds insufficient evidence to determine whether responsible beverage service (RBS) training programs reduce excessive alcohol consumption and related harms at the community level." The principle above is what the page says the training may teach. It is not a finding that training works, and it is not a rule of criminal procedure.
