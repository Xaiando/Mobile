# WSET sparkling and fortified closure sources

Authored and checked on 27 September 2026. The curriculum pack contains original explanations for the public Award in Wines Levels 2 and 3 scope. All new lessons remain unverified and exclude automatically generated multiple-choice distractors. Primary citations establish facts; conditional cost and quality applications are explicitly identified as authored reasoning rather than regulator promises about retail prices.

The Level 2 scope is the [2026 Issue 2.1 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf), learning outcome 5, printed/PDF page 14. The Level 3 scope is the live-linked [2022 Issue 2 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf), Unit 1 learning outcomes 3–4, printed pages 16–18 / PDF pages 18–20. The later issue information appears inside the Level 3 document despite the older cover wording.

The scope includes Champagne and its five named districts; Crémants d’Alsace, de Bourgogne and de Loire; Saumur and Vouvray; Asti, Prosecco and Conegliano Valdobbiadene; Cava; Sekt and Deutscher Sekt; and the specified sparkling regions in Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and California. Fortified coverage is Port, Sherry, Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise and Rutherglen Muscat. Madeira is optional for this qualification and is not promoted into a required denominator. Named labels and extraction methods are required concepts; every producer, vineyard or private quality category is not a separate compulsory geography target.

Currentness matters. The [MASAF consolidated Asti specification of 13 March 2026](https://www.masaf.gov.it/flex/cm/pages/ServeBLOB.php/L/IT/IDPagina/24256) supports current categories and continental hillside exposure. Classic white sweet Asti illustrates the single-fermentation method; the protected name does not guarantee that route or sweetness for every current product. The linked Gazette PDF has shifted text encoding, so its natural-factor discussion was checked using the recoverable encoded text in PDF pages 13–14. The pack does not transfer the smaller separate Canelli PDO’s boundary or climate rules to all Asti.

The current Jerez and Manzanilla specifications dated 21 May 2026 include wine and liqueur-wine categories. The pack accordingly qualifies classic fortified Fino routes instead of asserting that every current Sherry must receive spirit. The English board’s generic older pages contain older sweetness thresholds; those figures are not copied into the new Medium/Pale Cream lessons. Cava Reserva uses the current 18-month minimum, and Guarda Superior organic requirements are related to the 2025 harvest rather than a blanket assertion about all Cava already sold.

Regional growing evidence is explicit. Loire’s appellation guide distinguishes Vouvray’s autumn concentration opportunity from a sparkling harvest objective. Prosecco DOC article 9 describes Alpine shelter, Adriatic influence and late-season conditions. UPECB’s regional dossier describes varied Bourgogne sites and early base-wine picking. DWI connects German Riesling’s slow ripening and marked acidity with sparkling suitability. WoSA’s harvest account describes the earlier Cap Classique harvest; it does not prove that every South African source is coastal or cool.

Quality and price statements are conditional applications. Hand work on steep Asti and Conegliano Valdobbiadene sites can increase growing costs; tank processing can reduce individual bottle handling. Named traditional-method Crémant, Saumur, Vouvray and New World examples require sediment management, with longer lees inventory affecting space and capital. These applications combine the cited regional method with existing primary winery-cost evidence; they do not claim that every older bottle tastes better or sells for more. Generic tank-versus-bottle cases are separated from region-specific evidence in the JSON matrix.

The evidence JSON maps all 22 required style families to exact existing and new item IDs for grapes/style, growing environment, production, labels, and quality/price. Geographic identities and served practice must still pass the combined release checks. This source pack alone does not establish complete course coverage or a WSET qualification.

Final Level 2 boundaries are explicit. Transfer, ancestral and force-carbonation processes remain Level 3 required content; their optional lower-track mappings do not put them into the Level 2 completion denominator. The Level 2 country examples use the bounded Tasmania, Marlborough and Domaine Carneros explanations alongside traditional-method study. They teach Australia, New Zealand and USA sparkling context without declaring those particular regions additional compulsory Level 2 geographic families. Reserve Tawny is a contrast within general Tawny study, not a separately required Level 2 style category.

All 40 new source jurisdiction fields use actual publisher country codes. In particular, the Prosecco and current Asti regulations use IT, and the Cava and Manzanilla specifications use ES. The Port research published by the University of Trieste uses IT; Portuguese regulator and producer references use PT. Factual assertions and verification statuses are unchanged.
