# Additional WSET Level 3 grape roles and origin contexts

Authored 27 September 2026 against the live public [Level 3 Award in Wines specification, 2022 Issue 2](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf), Unit 1 LO2 Range 1–2. The batch fills 12 concrete missing contribution roles from the independent raw-assertion audit, strengthens Blaufränkisch/Zweigelt context, and adds the required Saumur-Champigny, Friuli Colli Orientali and Castilla y León IGP explanations. Every new point is original, remains unverified and has automatically generated MCQs disabled. Existing direct explanation questions continue to serve the points through the common principle templates.

Legal permission or berry colour alone was rejected as style evidence. Petit Verdot now has a late-ripening Bordeaux blending role; Graciano and Mazuelo have structural Rioja contributions; Sárga Muskotály has an aromatic role within sweet Tokaj. Existing grape profiles and permitted-grape assertions were reused rather than copied. The 14 grape entries in the evidence matrix include the 12 gaps plus the two Austrian refinements.

The [Portuguese regulator’s Jaen record](https://www.ivv.gov.pt/casta/jaen/), updated 19 February 2026, explicitly lists Mencía in Spain as its synonym. The pack therefore reuses `n_grape_mencia` and preserves the separate qualified Pirulé (Jaén, Ribera del Duero) legal entry. ViniPortugal and IVV give different general colour descriptions for Jaen; the authored lesson avoids a universal colour claim and teaches its fragrant blending contribution and balance instead. Argentine Bonarda is qualified in its identity and distinguished from Italian Bonarda names using Wines of Argentina’s first-party accounts.

The regional evidence JSON identifies exact origin nodes and five dimensions for each new origin family. Saumur-Champigny reuses its existing season, Loire thermal moderation and tuffeau-water mechanisms. Friuli Colli Orientali reuses its existing ponca/Alpine–Adriatic explanations and the current MASAF legal identity. The La Viarte technical description is evidence of a named producer’s steel/fine-lees route, not a compulsory treatment for every DOC wine. The regional wine board supports the broader white-variety range and typical comparisons.

The ITACYL Castilla y León specification is revision 0 of 19 December 2011, still available on the primary regulator’s site; the 2019 official regulation amendment is separately cited. This batch draws only broad protected-name, colour/category, variety range and stable natural-factor context from it. It does not reproduce outdated numeric yield, alcohol or legal-method thresholds. The high inland plateau explanation includes local variation and does not overwrite the Atlantic influence taught for Bierzo. Broad IGP status is kept separate from narrower DO labels and from a quality or price score.

Conditional extraction, lees, inventory and oak cost statements are authored applications of regional methods and existing primary general-production evidence. They do not equate lower yield, older wine, new oak or a protected name with better quality. Root integration must still establish actual serving modes, canonical mappings and combined coverage; this batch does not claim official qualification completion.
