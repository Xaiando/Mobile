# CMS spirits and liqueurs: named-example closeout (29 September 2026)

This area follows the [CMS Europe 2026/27 syllabus](https://courtofmastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syllabus-202627-1.pdf), printed pp. 24–25. Its Introductory column calls for a distillation principle; the Certified column names Islay, Mezcal, Marc/Grappa and fruit eau-de-vie and calls for identification and recommendation by liqueur flavour family. The app's selectable `CMS_CERTIFIED` track inherits `CMS_INTRODUCTORY`; these are not WSET Wines requirements. The syllabus sets scope, not product facts.

The new `cms_spirits_liqueurs_examples.yaml` adds 21 cited, expert-**unverified** items, nine direct recognition choices, three service-scenario choices and three four-criterion written cases. The four liqueurs are examples of individual products, not legal or sensory definitions of every liqueur in their family. Cream is a useful service contrast within the broader liqueur recommendation objective; the syllabus does not name cream as a separate Certified flavour family.

The Certified selectors explicitly include the nine new lesson/case parent nodes. Compared with the 0.24.11 predecessor, CMS has **2,878 versus 2,857 effective items**, **1,242 versus 1,221 core items**, and **1,950 versus 1,939 useful-practice items**; useful core practice rises from **812 to 823**. The spirits objective has **76 versus 63** selected items; liqueurs has **28 versus 20**. These are app inventory measures, not qualification or verified-content claims. The Introductory distillation item is inherited by Certified but mapped at depth 1; the 20 Certified items are depth 2. The refreshed baseline preserves zero blocking gaps and the Level 2/3 useful-core counts of **535/744** and **1,219/2,228**.

The Italian Grappa sheet is used for category recognition, not a claim that every bottle named Grappa is Italian: the [EU–Switzerland spirits agreement](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A22009A0530%2801%29) also allows the name for certain grape marc spirits from Italian-speaking Swiss areas. The practice therefore asks for the grape-marc category and checks the actual bottle label.

The earlier Irish Whiskey lessons were checked separately against [Ireland's published technical file](https://www.marketaccess.agriculture.gov.ie/media/marketaccess/content/Irish%20Whiskey%20Technical%20File.pdf), which still shows island-wide origin, three years in wooden casks up to 700 litres, and Pot Still, Malt, Grain and Blended distinctions. Ireland held a [2026 consultation on proposed amendments](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-agriculture-food-and-the-marine/consultations/public-consultation-on-proposed-amendments-to-the-irish-whiskey-technical-file/) that closed 4 September. No adopted amendment was found in this check, so the existing claims were not changed. Check the operative file again before release if that process advances.

The work closes the named examples identified in the September CMS audit. It does not verify the wider spirits inventory, tasting claims, actual cellar stock, exam readiness or learner mastery; those remain separate reviews. All new items retain `verification_status: unverified`.
