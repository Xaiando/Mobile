# CMS Certified non-classified liqueur example — 29 September 2026

The [CMS Europe 2026/27 syllabus](https://courtofmastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syllabus-202627-1.pdf), printed p. 25, lists “Non classified” alongside fruit, citrus, herb, bean/nut and flower under Certified liqueur knowledge and recommendation. It does not define that study heading as a legal category or name a required brand. The app's existing examples represented the other named flavours but did not explicitly give a practice example outside those families.

[Lucas Bols' 2023–24 annual report](https://www.lucasbols.com/files/6/2/1/8/2/The%20Lucas%20Bols%20Company%2023-24%20Annual%20Report_interactive.pdf), printed p. 42, describes **Zwarte Kip Advocaat** as a thick egg liqueur made with fresh free-range eggs and brandy. [Bols' liqueur guide](https://bols.com/blogs/news/history-and-production-of-liqueurs), “Types of liqueurs — Cream,” places Advocaat near cream styles and explains the egg-yolk-and-spirit basis. [Regulation (EU) 2019/787](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/787), Annex I category 39, separately defines egg liqueur or advocaat. That regulation defines a legal product category; it does **not** equate “advocaat” with the CMS syllabus's “Non classified” study heading. The app uses this named egg-led product as one useful recommendation contrast, not as a universal flavour, recipe or dietary rule.

The new CMS-only lesson, direct recognition choice, stock-conditional scenario choice and four-criterion written case all require checking the current bottle and asking about eggs before recommending it. The case contrasts a possible dairy-cream option with the egg-led example without claiming either is allergy-safe from a brand name. All five new assertions are `unverified` pending independent subject review, mapped only to `CMS_CERTIFIED` core depth 2, and excluded from generic MCQ generation.
