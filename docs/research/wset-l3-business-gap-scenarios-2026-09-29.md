# WSET Level 3 business application gaps — 29 September 2026

The [WSET Level 3 Award in Wines specification, Issue 2](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf) sets the scope of explaining how growing, production and commercial factors affect wine style, quality and price. This batch supplies four-option, item-specific practice for **30 previously unserved core `business` `PRINCIPLE_EXPLANATION` assertions** in the existing dataset. It adds no knowledge assertions, certification mappings or source-citation mappings. All 30 selected facts remain `unverified` and generic MCQs remain disabled; the new cues provide bounded cases for those facts rather than expert verification.

The cases test separation of fixed and volume costs, saleable-output denominators, actual parcel and lot expenses, constrained cellar capacity, release timing, and the distinction between cost recovery and customer willingness to pay. Figures, buyer briefs, trial results and operational constraints are explicit hypothetical premises, not reported regional averages. A region's name, challenging vineyard or extra production expense is never treated as proof of sensory quality or a price premium. Correct option positions are 8/8/7/7; each question has four distinct options, and no key is uniquely shortest or longest. Those mechanical checks do not establish exam-level difficulty.

## Primary-source ledger and limits

Each choice cites an existing source ID attached to its fact. The cited publisher evidence and the inference allowed in the question are:

| Existing source | Bounded application in the new choices |
| --- | --- |
| [U.S. Small Business Administration: Plan your business](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) | Break-even planning and separation of fixed from variable expenses. The bottling contract and 0.30-per-bottle charge are hypothetical. |
| [Australian Government: Choose a pricing strategy](https://business.gov.au/products-and-services/choose-a-pricing-strategy) | Revisit cost, margin, customer value and competing offers when inputs change. Higher cost alone does not prove an achievable higher selling price. |
| [Penn State Extension: Grape and wine business management](https://extension.psu.edu/food-safety-and-quality/grape-and-wine-production/business-management-and-marketing) | Winery planning and lot-specific finance/marketing checks. It is a resource collection, not evidence that a named winery used the supplied barrel, sorting or holding plan. Direct automated retrieval may return 403; publisher-indexed descriptions were checked. |
| [Penn State Extension: Wine Grape Production](https://extension.psu.edu/wine-grape-production) | Site-specific establishment investment and operating budgets. Historic example costs are not imported into the scenarios. |
| [Wine Australia: Planning your wine](https://www.wineaustralia.com/growing-making/winery-management/planning-your-wine) | Whole-business planning from desired wine through vineyard/cellar inputs, price and margins. It does not rank the named regions or guarantee returns from longer ageing. |
| [Eisenberg DAC](https://www.eisenberg-dac.at/en/home/) and [INAO: historical Collioure specification](https://extranet.inao.gouv.fr/fichier/CDC-Coll-111207.pdf) | Narrow/steep Eisenberg sites and rarely mechanisable Collioure terraces motivate recording actual hand-work and access/maintenance expenses. The Collioure document is historical context, not a claim about current appellation rules. Neither terrain source proves bottle quality or a premium. |
| [Vins de Provence: vineyard management](https://www.vinsdeprovence.com/en/le-rose/la-conduite-du-vignoble) | Cool incoming fruit can lower cellar cooling energy; the scenario asks for a net comparison with actual night labour and logistics. |
| [Wines of Argentina: 2020 harvest report](https://blog.winesofargentina.com/destacadas/argentina-2020-harvest-report/) and [Patagonia producer/regional profiles](https://blog.winesofargentina.com/es/noticias/vinos-patagonia-argentina/) | A producer's compressed 2020 intake motivates capacity planning, and regional wind context motivates checking a plot's actual shelter/access needs. Neither is a timeless harvest calendar or province-wide cost survey. |
| [Comité Champagne: Riddling](https://www.champagne.fr/en/about-champagne/how-champagne-is-made/riddling) | Mechanised riddling can bring bottle sediment to the neck. The case supplies the large run and labour constraint; it does not claim a particular producer's method or current regulatory obligation. |
| [Wine Australia: Murray Darling](https://www.wineaustralia.com/market-insights/regions-and-varieties/victoria-wines/murray-darling) | Fruit-led Shiraz/Cabernet may fit the stated buyer brief; taste and quote the offered lot rather than impose a universal regional price or style. |
| [Wine Country Ontario: Icewine](https://winecountryontario.ca/icewine/) | Frozen-fruit Icewine process is context for a hypothetical yield/cost calculation. The 24,000-unit expense and litre yields are invented for arithmetic and are not production or market data. |
| [Washington State Wine Commission: Grapes, Soil, & Sun](https://www.washingtonwine.org/grapes-soil-sun/) | Irrigation in the dry growing context prompts accounting for the actual system's water, energy and maintenance. It does not establish a statewide per-bottle cost. |

The regional labels in the other South African, Chilean, New Zealand, Californian and Canadian prompts are inherited from their existing assertions and linked regional sources. Their added cellar, fruit-selection, water, buyer and capacity conditions are deliberately supplied in the question and are evaluated with the linked business-planning source, rather than asserted as typical for a region. Existing source maps for the facts remain intact.
