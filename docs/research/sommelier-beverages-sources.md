# Sommelier beverage sources

Release 0.22.0. Twenty-two study points in `assets/curriculum/areas/sommelier_beverages.yaml`. All are unverified. They are mapped to CMS only. They are not on WSET Levels 1–3, and they are not on the Diploma denominator. Codex's Level 3 work is unchanged by them.

The points cover the EU split between spirit drinks and beer, wine and cider; rum, gin and vodka as adopted categories; Scotch identity, single malt and age statements; bourbon and straight bourbon; Cognac's minimum age, the official ageing mentions and Fine Champagne; cask-conditioned beer; UK excise cider and perry; sake polishing ratio, junmai, ginjo, daiginjo and honjozo; the Habanos denomination and cigar construction; and the World Health Organization statements on tobacco and alcohol.

Corrections that the wording is meant to keep:

- Scotch must be matured in Scotland for at least three years. An age statement is the youngest whisky, in years, not an average.
- Bourbon is at least 51 percent corn, stored in charred new oak, and distilled and aged in the United States. Kentucky is not required. Straight bourbon is aged at least two years and has no added coloring or flavoring.
- On the BNIC list, Napoleon is an ageing mention of at least six years. It is not Napoleon I, and it is not the 1855 classification. VS is 2 years, VSOP is 4, XO is 10 and XXO is 14. Each is the youngest eau-de-vie.
- Junmai has no added brewer's alcohol, and the old 70 percent polishing ceiling was removed. Ginjo is 60 percent or less remaining and daiginjo is 50 percent or less remaining. Honjozo may include brewer's alcohol, limited to 10 percent of the white-rice weight. Koji rice is at least 15 percent. The labelling page does not require hot service.
- UK excise "cider" covers cider and perry inside a strength band. It is not a world definition and it is not a spirit.
- Habanos is a Cuban denomination. A cigar made elsewhere is not a Habano, and not every Cuban cigar is one.
- Tobacco has no safe level. Ethanol is a carcinogen and the cited WHO statement gives no safe drinking threshold.

Not written, because the page was not in hand: a transcription of the 1516 Bavarian barley, hops and water decree. It is deliberately absent. It must not be taught as current world beer law. Tequila's agave percentage and a statutory parejo/figurado contrast were also left out. No home-distillation steps, cuts or still plans are included. The 94.8 percent and 96 percent figures are legal category ceilings, not a method.
