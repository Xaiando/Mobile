# Wine history sources

Release 0.22.0. Fifteen study points in `assets/curriculum/areas/wine_history.yaml`. All are unverified. They are mapped to Diploma and CMS, not to WSET Levels 1–3, so the Level 3 count stays 2,392. They use `PRINCIPLE_EXPLANATION` in the service domain. None is a map pin.

The points are the earliest cited Georgian grape-wine residues, the Areni-1 installation, Pliny on Falernian, the Methuen duty treaty of 27 December 1703, Merrett's 1662 sparkling note, the Dom Pérignon attribution, the 1756 Douro charter, Napoleon I's Berlin Decree, the 1855 ranking under Napoleon III, phylloxera and resistant rootstocks, the controlled-appellation conditions tied to the decree of 30 July 1935, US Prohibition and its repeal, the 24 May 1976 Paris tasting, and the current EU definition of wine.

Corrections that the wording is meant to keep:

- Areni-1, around 4000 calibrated BCE, is a production installation. It is not the oldest chemical evidence of grape wine. The Georgian residues, about 6000–5800 BC, are older, and they are not a complete winery.
- The 1855 ranking was requested by Napoleon III for the Paris exhibition. Brokers used reputation and recent prices. It covers Médoc reds, Haut-Brion, and Sauternes and Barsac. It does not cover Saint-Émilion or Pomerol. Napoleon I's Berlin Decree of 21 November 1806 is a trade blockade, not that ranking.
- The Douro charter of 10 September 1756 created a regulated company demarcation. Chianti in 1716 and Tokaj in 1737 were earlier delimited zones, so Douro is not taught here as the first line ever drawn around vines.
- Merrett described sugar and molasses making wine sparkling on 17 December 1662. The Royal College of Physicians account does not make Dom Pérignon the inventor of that method.
- Phylloxera control is cited from University of California guidance: resistant American rootstocks. This pass does not date the European epidemic, because that page does not.

Not written in this pass, because a page that would support the sentence was not in hand: Columella's vineyard book, Eleanor of Aquitaine and the Gascon trade, and the Italian DOC law of 1963. The 1935 point uses the wine code's later statement of that decree's conditions, not a standalone transcription of every article. The Methuen point uses the published treaty text, not a claim about how much Port was shipped. They should be widened only with a public page, not from memory.

The EU wine definition reuses the existing citation `src_eu_reg_1308_2013`. No new geography layer was added.
