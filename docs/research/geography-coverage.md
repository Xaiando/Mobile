## Implemented map questions

| Format | What the learner does | What is reviewed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `map_locate` | Finds one named area by tapping or choosing its name | Its canonical location item |
| `map_identify` | Names a highlighted area | The same canonical location item |
| `map_pair` | Finds two to four named places in a common frame, revising each choice before submission | Each requested location item independently; existing composite-review rules apply |
| `map_grape` | Finds an area matching one to three legal grape clues, or a separate dated planting record | Each requested fact independently; every represented alternative in the same cited legal or survey cohort is accepted |

These formats share FSRS memory with the corresponding text facts. Grape combinations intersect current permissions for the whole answer, while each requested fact receives its own grade on a partial answer. Co-items must be current authored cards served by the same template and mapped at the required depth in the active track; structural relations establish alternatives without becoming bonus reviews. Named-place co-items come from the active certification's effective mappings and served depth; a primary without enough mapped peers is ineligible. Seeds fix the selected peers and wording. All map answer views have a name-list alternative.

The dated planting family adds six complete **top-two planted-variety records for 2025**, observed in NZ Winegrowers' 2026 report: Marlborough, Central Otago, Hawke's Bay, Gisborne, Nelson and Wairarapa. Each statistic has a distinct dated subject linked to its geographic area; the source and observation year define its answer cohort. Hawke's Bay and Gisborne both rank Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay first/second. Questions say planted area and 2025; they do not use forecast or producing hectares, assert legal permissions, or establish every place where the grapes grow. [Source and measure](../../tool/geography/new_world_plantings_sources.md).

Locate and named-place questions use the existing progression from labelled to outline, minimal and blank modes. Grape maps keep selectable areas visible in labelled or outline mode. Point markers remain reference locations at any zoom. An exact tap on a requested area is accepted where candidate shapes overlap; a merely nearby target does not override an exact hit on another area. Identical or overlapping reference markers do not imply identical legal territories.

## Grape-permission safeguards

Grape maps require a current, cited, complete forward `PERMITS_GRAPE` set for every selectable area. Each assertion covers the complete permitted union, including principal, accessory and conditional varieties. The grader reads current structural relations as well as authored cards, so an allowed grape remains a correct answer even when its permission has no separate study item.

The snapshot has **30 asserted complete legal sets**:

- France: Chablis, Petit Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru, Chablis Grand Cru, Volnay, Cornas, Condrieu, Champagne, Pauillac, Sauternes, Bouzeron, Saint-Bris, Savennières, Sancerre, Morgon and Cahors.
- Italy: Barolo, Barbaresco and Brunello di Montalcino.
- Hungary and Greece: Tokaj, Nemea and Naoussa.
- Spain: Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Rías Baixas, Rueda, Toro, Priorat, Cava and Jerez.

The Spanish expansion adds 100 permission cards. Rías Baixas includes Ratiño Gallega in its current 15-variety union; Rueda's current 18-variety register excludes Godello and retains Palomino Fino only from existing plantings. Ribera includes six historical interplanting exceptions with the pre-21 July 1982 condition; its headline six-grape list cannot establish prohibition. Cava retains Trepat's rosé restriction. Rioja Turruntés reuses Albillo Mayor, while Malvasía Riojana / Subirat Parent reuse Alarije; Torrontés (Galicia) and Toro's Malvasía Castellana remain distinct. Qualified Pirulé (Jaén, Ribera del Duero) makes no unsupported identity claim with Portuguese Jaen/Mencía.

Physical berry-colour facts cite primary cultivar catalogues and use flashcard/typed recall. Gewürztraminer is pink, and Pinot Gris is grey, even though both make white wine. The inherited Pinot Gris white metadata is preserved with an expiry date; current typed recall accepts Grey/Gray and rejects the retired White answer. Its sparse grey-grape pool disables five permission/planting MCQs while retaining other practice. Unresolved Pirulé and an unknown required colour cannot supply matching MCQ rivals. [Correction and primary source](../../tool/geography/grape_colour_corrections_sources.md).

Legal grape-map coverage is limited to the 30 complete unions above. Beyond the six New Zealand ranking records, further New World, British, Chinese and other regional plantings, characteristic-variety comparisons and climate/site clues remain Diploma authoring work; a reference-location marker does not establish a complete grape-growing curriculum.

These are permission lists, not a complete map of where grapes are grown. Champagne's June 2026 specification includes Chardonnay rose and conditional Voltis; the authored explanation retains the experimental condition. Austrian DAC headline varieties do not form a complete union when other quality-wine grapes are allowed. Santorini also permits further minor varieties in Vinsanto. Neither receives a completeness assertion. Other partially authored permissions, including Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Château-Chalon and Madiran, cannot supply absence-based wrong answers.

Specifications that distinguish principal and accessory varieties retain those relations. Neutral `PERMITS_GRAPE` unions support the complete legal set without changing the classifications. Track mappings govern study selection; they do not make a legally correct represented alternative incorrect.

## Geometry and sources

The generated bundle contains **31 layers, totalling 2516.1 KB** against the 8192 KB budget. Country outlines and physical context come from Natural Earth. Existing French region/appellation polygons use INAO production-commune lists and IGN administrative outlines. Those polygons show geographical production areas rather than planted vineyards; informal combinations do not acquire a new statutory boundary.

The new marker layers use separately pinned, openly licensed snapshots:

- World and European points: Wikidata coordinates under CC0. Some are wine-region gazetteer entries; others are named municipality/locality references. Per-feature source identity, coordinate claim, role and retrieval metadata preserve that distinction. [World source notes](../../tool/geography/world_atlas_sources.md).
- Selected French vineyard points: representative points derived from INAO's 21 September 2026 parcel data under Open Licence 2.0. The source parcels remain informational; approved specifications and delimitation plans determine legal eligibility. [French source notes](../../tool/geography/france_atlas_sources.md).
- Six further Chablis Premier Cru reference points: interior points of named June 2026 cadastral lieux-dits, matched through INAO's name table. Vaulorent and Beauroy use named constituent lieux-dits. The source publishes the original Open Licence 1.0, which is preserved rather than relabelled as version 2.0. [Cadastral derivation and matches](../../tool/geography/france_chablis_cadastre_sources.md).
- Champagne traditional areas: a mix of Wikidata coordinates and labelled IGN commune reference points, disclosed in their feature metadata.
- Historical French commune reconciliation: Insee's 2026 commune-event list under Open Licence 1.0. Reconciliation must not silently enlarge an appellation into its modern successor commune; unresolved cases retain a sourced point fallback.
- The Chablis/Alsace completion adds 47 INAO parcel-derived points (Open Licence 2.0), 23 cadastral points (Open Licence 1.0) and a separately qualified CC0 Côte de Fontenay municipality reference. [Completion methods and matches](../../tool/geography/france_atlas_completion_sources.md).
- Further European reference markers retain the exact Wikidata coordinate statements and CC0 licence. [European source notes](../../tool/geography/europe_atlas_completion_sources.md).
- New World AVA interior references derive from the UC Davis AVA Project's pinned CC0 polygons; Wikidata supplies other CC0 locations. Thirteen locality/terrain references use the GeoNames national gazetteers under CC BY 4.0, with their own source, layer, edition/hash and attribution. [New World source notes](../../tool/geography/new_world_atlas_completion_sources.md).
- The Australian/South African expansion uses officially licensed Wine Australia CC BY 4.0 interior references, Wikidata CC0 and GeoNames CC BY 4.0, with exact register inventories. [Sources and register matching](../../tool/geography/new_world_oceania_africa_sources.md).
- Canadian, Chilean, Argentine and further US additions retain primary legal facts and separate licensed references. Complete original Natural Earth 10m island parts restore Salt Spring Island and Rapa Nui in their country backgrounds. [Americas inventories, homonym checks and island derivation](../../tool/geography/new_world_americas_sources.md).

- Diploma source notes cover [Britain](../../tool/geography/british_atlas_sources.md), [China](../../tool/geography/china_atlas_sources.md), [New York](../../tool/geography/diploma_us_atlas_source.md) and the [additional regional frames](../../tool/geography/diploma_regions_sources.md). Administrative areas, wine GIs and informal areas retain their distinct types.

Points show **locations, not wine-area boundaries**. Municipal points are not claimed to be appellation centroids; parcel and cadastral markers are not legal climat polygons. The map displays the point qualification, visible-layer attributions are available in the map, and Settings → About lists the source citations and licences.

The pipeline registry, layer recipes, checked-in GeoJSON snapshots and hashes make builds reproducible. See [`sources.yaml`](../../tool/geography/sources.yaml), [`layers.yaml`](../../tool/geography/layers.yaml) and the generated [build report](../../tool/geography/report.md). No copyrighted wine-map tracing or OSM-derived geometry is introduced by this expansion. Licence questions remain in [L-15, L-25 and the atlas entries](../legal-review.md).
