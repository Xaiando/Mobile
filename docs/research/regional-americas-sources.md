## Comparison reasoning

The cases' parcel availability, sample descriptions, quotes, margin targets and order conditions are invented premises. Regional sources support the physical context, while allocation, value assessment and inventory tradeoffs are **conditional inferences** from those premises. Sources do not endorse those particular contracts, establish current prices or prove a unique best response. Citation locators explicitly distinguish the inferred parts.

The California case allocates grapes to stated style targets, while requiring actual site checks. The Pacific Northwest case compares Willamette and eastern Columbia Valley opportunities and makes the larger-order inventory tradeoff explicit. The Chile case uses a tasted sample to justify freshness rather than assigning flavour with certainty from a region or soil. The Mendoza case refuses a quality/price ranking based solely on altitude and flags unmatched harvest and oak practices as confounders. No case infers blind origin certainty from aromas.

MCQs are disabled because overlapping climate, grape, sourcing and winemaking alternatives can be defensible. Typed aliases recall concise concepts; an accepted alias does not demonstrate that the learner explained a mechanism or evaluated the complete case.
