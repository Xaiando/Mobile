Authored on 27 September 2026 for `DIP-3`, using original prompts and public primary references. This is a selected analytical continuation, not a complete regional course. All 44 items remain **unverified**, with qualified review outstanding. The module adds 14 two-point comparisons and four conditional cases; it adds no place nodes, geographic edges or geometry. The existing atlas supplies separate location practice.

The area file contains 62 nodes, 44 relations, 44 knowledge items, 100 track mappings, 44 aliases, 16 new source records and 57 citations. All 44 points map to WSET Level 4 core at depth 3. The 28 principle points also map to WSET Level 3 and CMS Europe Certified core at depth 2; the 16 case points are Diploma only. Domains are assigned by the actual subject matter, including commercial and vineyard mechanisms; the root release integration explicitly assigns this regional module to D3 learner progress.

All new points disable MCQ because qualified site and style explanations and defensible case decisions admit alternatives. Short answers use the existing self-check format. Case nodes contain the entire hypothetical scenario so that individual flashcard and typed reviews retain the premises.

## Editorial comparisons and original cases

Regional-body descriptions provide observed growing conditions, local varieties and broad style tendencies. Joining those descriptions into a comparison is an **editorial inference**, made explicit in each citation locator when needed. Climatic explanations are qualified: temperature, aspect, exposure, soil water supply, harvest and cellar treatment can interact. The Clare acid-retention point generalises the page’s documented cooling mechanism into a Riesling comparison; it does not assert that every Clare Riesling retains the same acidity.

The four cases supply invented premises and ask for a defensible comparison, mechanism, tradeoff and limitation. No actual market price or comparative quality ranking is asserted. The Australian Shiraz case holds vintage and maturation broadly similar but acknowledges remaining variables. The Cabernet price case uses supplied higher costs and similar sensory evaluations to distinguish cost recovery from customer value. The New Zealand white case deliberately changes both grape and cellar treatment, requiring the learner to recognise that the pair cannot isolate a regional effect. The Chenin case states customer preference and budget, requiring a trial rather than a region-based quality hierarchy.

The generic two-point comparison pools and four scoped case pools remain self-checked practice. They do not automate an official Diploma essay assessment or prove blind-origin identification.
