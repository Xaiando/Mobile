# Original tasting teaching and calibration

39 concise lessons in 13 groups teach Level 1 observation and structural sensations, Level 2 descriptive detail and aroma development, and Level 3 evidence for quality and readiness. Direct mappings use the lowest appropriate WSET level and inherit cumulatively. They remain `unverified`: this is internal source review, not qualified expert verification.

Scope follows the current public Wines specifications: [Level 1, June 2022 Issue 1.2](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11682/wset_l1wines_spec_en_jun2022_issue12.pdf), [Level 2, 2026 Issue 2.1](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf), and [Level 3, May 2022 Issue 2](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf). Level 1/2 tasting is teaching, not a tasting examination. Level 3 requires physical assessed tasting, which app practice cannot certify.

Teaching sources, accessed 27 September 2026:

- [WSET tasting skill introduction](https://www.wsetglobal.com/knowledge-centre/blog/2019/november/how-to-build-your-wine-tasting-skills): observation, familiar associations, structure and comparison.
- [WSET mouthfeel](https://www.wsetglobal.com/knowledge-centre/blog/2026/the-secret-language-of-wine-what-is-mouthfeel): alcohol, acidity, tannin, weight and gas. Its unsupported universal statements are excluded.
- [WSET acidity](https://www.wsetglobal.com/knowledge-centre/blog/2026/understanding-acidity-in-wine): mouthwatering sensation and balance; no universal malolactic assertion is repeated.
- [WSET ageing](https://www.wsetglobal.com/knowledge-centre/blog/2023/march/21/why-do-we-age-wine): intended early drinking, supporting structure, development and uncertainty. Legal examples are excluded.
- [Public 2018 WSET candidate guide](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/6714/wset_l4diploma_cag_tasting_en_aug2018.pdf): concise general aroma categories and quality concepts only. It is not used as current scope or grading authority. No grid, marking scheme or worked tasting example is reproduced.
- [AWRI assessment course notes](https://www.awri.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/01-AWAC-Course-Notes-28092022-1.pdf): bias, preference, discussion, consistency and the limits of wine size/intensity as quality evidence. No scoring table is reproduced.
- [Wine Australia tannin](https://www.wineaustralia.com/growing-making/vineyard-management/tannin): astringency and bitterness.
- Existing canonical OIV sensory and AWRI faults citations are reused; their URLs are not duplicated.

The three new grids use original headings and app vocabulary. The unchanged database stores them under the legacy `WSET_SAT` track enum; that storage value does not identify a copied official grid. Existing `tg_structured` and `tg_deductive` definitions and saved vocabulary stay intact. The app grids are not an official SAT and do not imply an official examination score.

Nine fictional calibration scenarios have three difficulty levels and describe every required observation field. Their delayed feedback is app-authored discussion guidance. Reference conclusions can be challenged with evidence; self-checked criteria are subjective declarations. Physical-wine mode records observations and written evidence without claiming automatic correctness. Cases link exact current, level-mapped teaching IDs. Snapshots and notes remain available through backup/restore.

Independent content review and broader runtime validation remain tracked in the completion ledger; adding these artifacts does not by itself close all WSET study coverage.
