# Independent general-production outcome review

Reviewed 27 September 2026 against the live [Level 2 April 2026 Issue 2.1 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf), Outcomes 1–2, and the current [Level 3 May 2022 Issue 2 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf), Unit 1 Outcome 1. Scope documents determine educational requirements; separate primary references support wine-production assertions. This is internal editorial/source review, not expert verification or official qualification approval. No SDK or Git commands were run.

The reviewer read all 67 new `ki_wset_prod_*` explanations and their evidence ledger, all existing 40 vineyard and 42 winery mechanism lessons, the foundation pack, relevant tasting/grape lessons and effective Level 2/3 mappings across registered includes. Catalog counts alone were not treated as outcome coverage. Additional general lessons and explicit lower-level reuse are needed before closing these production outcomes.

## Concrete outcome gaps

| Gap | Existing evidence and limitation | Required repair |
|---|---|---|
| Level 2 bottle-ageing comparison | `ki_wset_taste_ageing_evolution`, `_not_all`, `_uncertain` are Level 3 only. Hunter Sémillon and Shiraz supply individual developed flavour examples; Nebbiolo has a grape-specific colour reference. There is no general red/white bottle-ageing colour lesson at Level 2. | Add conditional red/white colour evolution and promote the existing beginner evolution/limits lessons. Do not imply that every wine improves or that colour identifies an exact age. |
| Level 2 vine-age and harvest labels | Old-vine mentions occur in Level 3 producer cases, without generic label meaning. Climate/weather and vintage Champagne do not teach the generic harvest-year-versus-bottling-year distinction. `ki_wset_eu_alsace_vt_sgn` is Level 3 only. | Teach vintage, late harvest and old-vine wording; promote the existing Alsace VT/SGN explanation rather than duplicate it. Acknowledge the OIV 2024 old-vine definition while distinguishing recommendations/certifier schemes from worldwide label enforcement. |
| Level 2 origin vocabulary | France/Italy/Spain traditional terms already have suitable Level 2 lessons. German `ki_landwein_protection`, `ki_qualitaetswein_protection`, `ki_praedikatswein_protection` are Level 3 only. PDO/PGI occur in individual labels without a general scheme comparison. | Reuse the German identity facts at Level 2, and add an original introductory GI/PDO/PGI comparison. Define terms in the relevant jurisdiction; a protected origin is not an automatic sensory ranking. |
| Level 2 ripeness effects beyond sugar/acid | Foundation sugar/acid, veraison and harvest sampling are present. Regional Sauvignon/Cabernet comparisons supply isolated ripeness associations, but the general white/black flavour and tannin development required by Outcome 1 is too implicit. | Add conditional white/black aroma progression and phenolic-maturity interpretation. Avoid a universal white-tropical/red-black-fruit rule and avoid treating Brix or seed colour as proof of complete ripeness. |
| Level 3 vine structure | `ki_vit_current_shoots`, `_spring_reserves` and `ki_wset_prod_spur_cane` refer to shoots/wood, but the named green-parts/one-year-wood/permanent-wood distinction is not taught directly. | Add a compact structural comparison linking current green growth, retained last-season canes and permanent trunk/arms. |
| Level 2 oak-variable mechanisms | `ki_win_oak_variables` names size/age/toast and `_reuse` supplies declining extractable oak. A list of variables does not explain why small/large vessels or different heating affect the wine. | Add original size/contact and toast explanations, with wine/wood/time controlled and no automatic small/new/heavily toasted quality ranking. |
| Level 2 blending complexity | `ki_wset_prod_blend_balance` and `_consistency` explain two aims; the explicit complexity aim is not explained. | Add a short complementary-layer example rather than claim more varieties guarantee complexity or repair spoilage. |
| Level 3 extraction terminology | Cold soak, cap contact and post-fermentation maceration are present. Regional producer notes name pump overs/punch downs without explaining the operations. | Add definitions of pumping liquid over the cap and plunging the cap into liquid, connected to extraction/control and intended style. |

These are teaching/mapping repairs, not a requirement to clone every existing fact. Root authorised a disjoint `wset_outcome_closure.yaml` pack with source/evidence/test files. Existing packs remain unchanged.

## Present material and acceptable scope decisions

Vine resources, annual cycle, crop balance, representative harvest sampling, frost inversion limits and sprinkler freezing heat, severe water stress, sunburn, drainage and fungal hazards are already substantive mechanism teaching. New training/pruning taxonomy, hail/animal protection, winter hilling, input-management approaches and harvest choices close important Level 3 details. Conditional caveats do not replace the positive mechanism: the facts normally explain both what happens and when the inference is limited.

Winery coverage already distinguishes crushing/pressing, white skin contact, fermentation/yeast health, extraction stages, intracellular versus conventional fermentation, malolactic conversion, oxygen/SO2 roles, lees, adjustments, vessel choices, blending, clarification, microbial/tartrate stability, packaging and closures. Cost/price explanations correctly condition on usable volume, resources and market demand. The educational steps remain original rather than copied official diagrams or questions.

I found no clearly wrong Level 2 promotion in the new pack. Detailed clones, training/pruning forms, biodynamic certification, technical filter/closure taxonomy and winter hilling remain Level 3; introductory adjustments, rosé/sweet routes, harvest choice and blending legitimately support Level 2. Regional geography pins and simple permission facts are not accepted as mechanistic production evidence. Named regional style/quality/price applications remain a separate catalog audit, not closed by the 24 general dimensions in the evidence ledger.

## Representative primary-source checks

- [AWRI carbonic maceration](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/winemaking_resources/winemaking-practices/winemaking-treatment-carbonic-maceration/), opening mechanism paragraphs: supports the intact-berry anaerobic phase, limited intracellular alcohol and distinction from mixed/semi-carbonic processes. The app does not imply an entire finished wine is yeast-free.
- [UC Davis FPS history/nomenclature](https://fps.ucdavis.edu/grapebook/winebook.cfm?chap=FPSEstablished), Nomenclature for grape clones and selections: supports within-variety vegetative selections and the caution that a source number does not certify superior performance.
- [UK wine-production guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/wine-production/wine-production), separate enrichment/sweetening headings: supports their distinction. The app deliberately does not repeat old numerical UK limits as current worldwide permission.
- [NMSU grapevine phenology](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/_h/H338/), annual growth/ripening: supports green shoot development, reserves, flowering/fruit set and changing sugar. The new green/wood lesson will use these actual plant mechanisms, not a scope PDF as a factual reference.
- [WSET ageing explanation](https://www.wsetglobal.com/knowledge-centre/blog/2023/march/21/why-do-we-age-wine), development and immediate-consumption sections: supports changing primary character, developed aromas, potentially softer tannin and the fact that prolonged ageing does not benefit every wine. The proposed lower-level reuse remains descriptive rather than an official judgement of ageworthiness.
- [European Commission quality schemes](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes-explained_en), wine PDO/PGI sections: supports protected-origin distinctions; wine-specific rules must be distinguished from the general food-product explanations.
- [OIV resolution 703-2024](https://www.oiv.int/fr/node/3806), adopted old-vine/old-vineyard definitions: establishes an international recommended definition. It must not be flattened into either “no definition exists anywhere” or a claim that every country's label is automatically governed by this recommendation.

No direct factual defect was found in these representative checks of the 67 new production statements. This finding does not upgrade their `unverified` status. The closure pack and integrated required catalog must receive ingestion, mapping/currentness, served-practice, progress and release checks before an internal completion flag is enabled. The app must keep study coverage, learner practice, fact memory and a separately recorded official result distinct.
