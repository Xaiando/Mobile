# Beginner outcome closure

Nine original lessons supplement the existing foundation pack with ordered winemaking routes, step definitions, cool/warm ripening and four familiar red-wine style examples. Five explicit Level 1 mappings reuse existing umami, intensity and tasted-flavour lessons. This followed the independent outcome review; it is internal editorial review, not qualified factual verification. Facts remain unverified and generic MCQs disabled.

Each row in `wset_level_1_closure.yaml` has source-specific locators. University of Georgia C717 supplies crushing, juice separation, red/white fermentation and bottling mechanisms, not its outdated legal, home-recipe, universal ageing, tannin-addition or minimum-alcohol advice. Rioja's current board pages supply maturation, bottling and contrasting young/aged styles; no universal malolactic or oak claim is adopted. Rosé is explicitly one common short-contact route, with alternatives acknowledged. NMSU phenology supplies temperature and ripening effects. Extreme heat and water stress prevent a universal warm-equals-riper rule.

Chianti's identikit and CMS's public Sangiovese markers supply typical sensory/structure examples. Inter Rhône's [Côtes du Rhône](https://www.vins-rhone.com/en/rhone-valley-vineyards/appellations/aoc-cotes-du-rhone), Grenache and Châteauneuf-du-Pape pages supply typical blend/style context. Promotional health and guaranteed-quality claims are excluded. Rioja's Tempranillo page supports young fruit versus developed vanilla/cocoa associations. Existing canonical citations are reused except the distinct Côtes du Rhône page.

The unreleased app Level 1 observation grid now appends original tasted-flavour vocabulary; historical grids remain untouched. All three fictional Level 1 calibration notes describe tasted flavours and ask for evidence. Physical wine practice is optional for Levels 1–2; the app's milestone requires original calibration participation instead. The official specifications permit non-sampling learners. Level 3 keeps two-wine practice but the app does not assess or award an official tasting qualification.
