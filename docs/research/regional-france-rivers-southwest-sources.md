## Original cases and inference boundaries

The Muscadet case explicitly supplies a split base wine, observed texture, comparable treatment, six/eighteen-month trial choices, occupied vats and delayed sales. The periods are hypothetical and do not assert legal sur-lie minima. Lees support the possible style contribution; the stated capacity/payment premises and general cash-flow source support the commercial tradeoff. The trial does not prove future demand or quality superiority.

The Rhône case supplies measured maturity relative to a producer's chosen target and worsening block-specific water constraint. Source timing/adaptation and technical monitoring justify separate assessment, not a numeric harvest prescription or irrigation permission. Different harvesting operations are an original logistical consequence; no labour-cost amount or unavoidable crop-loss outcome is claimed.

The Madiran case supplies two extraction trials, current sensory differences, a restaurant preference and absent mature evidence. A provisional customer-fit choice is supported; contact time is not established as the sole cause, and extra colour or tannin is not proof of quality or ageing success. Maceration/extraction methods are drawn from the local producer body's technical account, not copied examination material.

The Jurançon/Monbazillac case supplies the actual concentration processes, comparable current tasting suitability and documented **lot-specific** extra harvest labour. Regional references support process plausibility, while the premise supplies the comparative cost. Government business sources support price/demand evaluation. The exercise does not claim that all noble-rot harvesting costs more, or that either process deserves a universal premium.
