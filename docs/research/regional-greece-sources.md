## Editorial study-level mapping

All 44 points map to WSET Level 4 core at depth 3. The [pinned August 2025 Diploma specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/17609/wset_l4wines_specification_en_august-2025.pdf), printed page 14, supports regional work across Macedonia, the Peloponnese and the islands; it does not certify this selected set as complete.

The [pinned 2022 Level 3 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf), printed pages 11 and 13, names Naoussa, Nemea and Santorini. Thirteen foundation points relevant to those named regions are core at depth 2. The other 15 are secondary supporting comparisons: Amyndeon site/soil context and its dated harvest example, Mantinia, Cefalonia, Samos and the Cretan principles. A whole-country selector does not make all of those additional regions mandatory at Level 3.

The [pinned CMS Europe 2026/27 syllabus](https://courtofmastersommeliers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Syllabus-202627-1.pdf), Greek section on PDF page 16 (zero-based page 15), includes regional climate, grapes, principal wines including Mantinia and Samos, and Greek production context. Sixteen foundations map to Certified core at depth 2: the 13 shared principal-region points, two Mantinia foundations and the Samos vineyard/grape point. Twelve supplementary foundations are secondary, including the named producer's dry Samos example. All 28 foundations therefore have both lower-track mappings; none is excluded. The 16 case points are Diploma-only. These are editorial scope judgements, not an official content endorsement.

## Access and inference limits

Official scope PDFs and regional/producer material were actually read through live browser text, indexed primary passages or direct live HTTP retrieval. Intermittent browser timeouts affected several Wines of Greece variety profiles. Direct HTTP 200 recovered Agiorgitiko, Assyrtiko, Xinomavro, Kotsifali and the Liatiko variety profile; indexed primary text also exposed the complete supporting Mandilaria, cooperative-Robola and newer Liatiko passages. The SantoWines English heritage page initially returned a browser 526 but subsequently returned HTTP 200 with its complete manual-work paragraph. Unused guessed English heritage/Robola PDO paths returned 404 and were discarded; no citation points to those paths. The cash-flow page was opened live, with a later browser timeout during a repeat fetch; direct HTTP 200 then recovered its current “Choose your payment terms”, “Manage your inventory” and “Negotiate with your suppliers” passages, which were read before freeze. These retrieval distinctions remain separate from qualified expert verification.

The northern case deliberately supplies different vintages, extraction and wood treatments. Its action requests better comparison controls; it cannot assign the observed texture solely to region. The Nemea case supplies actual panel fit, higher barrel/storage costs for the second offer and a constrained cash budget. Those premises, not a regional price rule, justify the provisional customer decision.

The Santorini case supplies a currently functioning exposed parcel and an untested taller trellis proposal, with conversion payable before harvest. Its trial recommendation does not assert that all trellises fail, that baskets are always optimal or that manual heritage commands a premium. The aromatic-white case explicitly identifies three still dry offers and provides analytical/tasting evidence, different textures, one lot-specific lower landed cost and unknown supply/demand. The named dry Samos example establishes plausibility; perfume never substitutes for analytical sweetness, and the case does not confer PDO Samos permission on a hypothetical dry wine.
