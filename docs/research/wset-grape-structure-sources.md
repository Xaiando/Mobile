# WSET Level 2 grape structure: source and requirement ledger

Authored 27 September 2026. This closes semantic dimensions in the thirty named grape rows; it does not mark the knowledge as expert verified. Existing profiles, variations, regional examples and generic physiology are reused rather than cloned. Eight principal grapes have vineyard/harvest and cellar/ageing evidence; the additional twenty-two have the LO4 colour, acid, sugar/potential-alcohol, tannin (black grapes) and aroma/style dimensions. All new items remain unverified and MCQ-disabled, core Level 2 at depth 2. Cumulative inheritance supplies Level 3 and Level 4.

Scope is the [current WSET Level 2 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf), 2026 issue 2.1, printed pages 9 and 12–13. The public 2023 session plan is used only for a short Cabernet/Merlot factual contrast, not as the current syllabus or an assessment bank. No official SAT table, worksheets, exam material or complete source profiles are copied.

## Interpretive limits

Finished wine colour is distinct from berry colour. Sugar in fruit provides potential alcohol; dry fermentation, residual sugar, concentration and cellar choices prevent a universal alcohol score for a grape. General warmth and ripeness effects are conditional on suitable growing conditions. Cultivar observations are qualified by region and site, maturity, yield and extraction. Cultivar acidity and typical regional finished-wine acidity can differ: Syrah is not universally high-acid, ripe Pinot Noir may lose acidity, and Hunter Sémillon deliberately differs from ripe broad Sémillon. No legal minimum is used as a typical sensory mean. Primary producer examples remain named, dated examples rather than national averages.

The CMS Grape Markers source is used selectively for the brief Pinot Noir, Syrah and Grenache contrasts and the already-used Nebbiolo context. Its erroneous Corvina section is explicitly excluded. Corvina instead uses the actual Tomasi primary research introduction. Barbera uses the consortium’s explicit few-tannins statement; anthocyanin-rich colour does not establish tannin quantity. Pinotage guidance supplies style-dependent extraction and sugar/acid checks, while the Southern Right 2020 example demonstrates fresh acidity; numeric winemaking recommendations are not mandatory law. Garganega uses only qualitative sensory/berry-balance discussion in the 2019/2020 consolidated specification, not a claim about current numerical production rules. Furmint’s gold colour is tied to the named Grand Tokaj 2024 wine.

### ki_wset_structure_chardonnay_structure

Chardonnay white wine can be pale yellow or deeper gold, depending on style and development. The grape can accumulate substantial sugar while retaining acidity; cooler or earlier-picked lots often have less potential alcohol and more acidity than riper comparable lots. Colour and body do not establish whether oak was used.

### ki_wset_structure_pinot_gris_structure

### ki_wset_structure_riesling_structure

### ki_wset_structure_cabernet_sauvignon_structure

At suitable maturity, Cabernet Sauvignon can give deeply coloured red wine with substantial tannin and naturally high acidity. Cooler or less ripe examples may retain greener aromas; riper suitable sites can supply more sugar and potential alcohol. Extraction, blending with softer Merlot and maturation alter the finished structure; no bottle is guaranteed a fixed level.

### ki_wset_structure_merlot_structure

Merlot can give richly coloured reds with supple tannin and lower acidity than a comparable Cabernet Sauvignon. Ripe fruit can contain substantial sugar and yield a fuller, higher-alcohol dry style. Cooler sites, harvest timing, extraction and blends can make lighter or firmer wines, so supple does not mean tannin-free.

### ki_wset_structure_pinot_noir_structure

A cool-site Pinot Noir red often has lighter ruby colour, fresh acidity and less tannin than Cabernet Sauvignon. Very ripe Pinot Noir can accumulate high sugar while losing acidity; warm or more extracted wines may be fuller and firmer. Thin skins and lighter colour do not prevent complexity, nor prove a wine is ready to drink.

### ki_wset_structure_syrah_structure

Syrah can make intensely coloured reds with substantial tannin. Cooler Northern Rhône expressions can have fresher acidity and less alcohol than ripe warm-site Shiraz; the cultivar can also give relatively low acidity at maturity. Sugar, harvest choice, extraction and blending change the balance, so deep colour does not guarantee high acidity.

### ki_wset_structure_gamay_structure

### ki_wset_structure_grenache_structure

Grenache can accumulate much sugar, supporting high alcohol in ripe dry wines, and often has relatively low acidity. It commonly gives lighter red colour and supple tannin; clone, yield and extraction can make more structured examples. Warm-site ripe fruit and blends explain why a Grenache-led wine need not be light-bodied.

### ki_wset_structure_nebbiolo_structure

Higher-sugar fruit raises potential alcohol and can yield more actual alcohol when fermented dry than a comparable lower-sugar lot.

### ki_wset_structure_zinfandel_structure

For red Zinfandel, skin contact supplies colour and tannin. Its unevenly ripening bunches can include less ripe berries that supply bright acidity beside ripe or raisined fruit. Riper high-sugar fruit supports greater potential alcohol, while soft or strongly extracted styles have different acid and tannin balances. An earlier rosé or sweet blush has a different colour and structure.

Pinotage can give deeply coloured reds with substantial tannin, but extraction can produce a softer early-release style. Ripe high-sugar fruit supports greater potential alcohol; acid and pH need checking rather than assuming one level. ### ki_wset_structure_carmenere_structure

Inadequate ripening can retain green flavours; suitable warm-site sugar accumulation can support a fuller, higher-alcohol dry wine, without fixing every Chilean bottle’s levels.

A balanced, concentrated Cabernet Sauvignon with sufficient structure can develop beyond fresh black fruit towards dried-fruit, earthy or tobacco-like complexity while tannin texture softens. Barrel-derived vanilla or cedar may already be present before bottle age. Deep colour and strong tannin alone do not establish that keeping this bottle will improve it.

### ki_wset_structure_merlot_ageing

A light, fruit-led Merlot may suit early drinking; a suitably concentrated and balanced structured wine can develop dried-fruit or tobacco-like complexity with softer tannin during ageing. Oak can add separate wood-derived aromas. Neither supple texture nor the Merlot name tells you that every bottle should be kept, and poor storage can spoil development.
