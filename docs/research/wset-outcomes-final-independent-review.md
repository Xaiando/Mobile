# Independent review: general outcomes and regional applications

Reviewed 27 September 2026 by the Level 1/2 research agent. This is an independent editorial review of other agents' content, not expert verification, WSET endorsement or confirmation of an award. No SDK tests or Git commands were run for this review. My own foundation, grape-profile and New World packs are excluded from the independent approval judgment.

## Scope and method

I compared the complete 20-point outcome-closure pack and its seven selected existing lessons, the 67-point general-production pack and its catalog references, the critic's 27 regional grape-role lessons, and the root's 19 European regional applications with the current official outcome ranges. I read assertions and level mappings rather than equating an ID, a citation or a map pin with instruction. I checked representative factual and legal sources directly and inspected their actual scope.

Scope references are the [2026 Issue 2.1 Level 2 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/19132/wset_l2wines_specification_en_april2026_issue21.pdf), printed pages 7–9, and [May 2022 Issue 2 Level 3 specification](https://www.wsetglobal.com/media/11731/wset_l3wines_specification_en_highres_may2022_issue2.pdf), Unit 1 LO1, printed pages 8–9. The specifications determine curriculum scope; factual and statutory assertions need separate appropriate sources. The PDF publication page describes Level 2 as 2026 Issue 2.1 (rebranded); an access date is not its original publication date.

## General outcomes

The current general-production and closure texts cover the named grape components, growth requirements, annual stages, maturation changes, climate/weather and site influences, vineyard decisions, concentration routes, fermentation, red/white/rosé and sweet routes, vessels, oak variables, malolactic conversion, lees, blending and bottle development. Level 3 additionally has vine species/clones/rootstocks, retained and green vine parts, training/pruning, hazards and disease management, extraction routes and packaging/closures. I found no remaining substantive omission in these general ranges after the closure additions; this conclusion does not approve the distinct grape-specific or geographic outcomes.

The closure lessons add useful explicit explanations of red and white colour development, vintage, old vines, late harvest, GIs/PDO/PGI, white and black ripeness aromas, phenolic maturity, retained versus current-season growth, oak size/toast, blending complexity and the two cap operations. The seven reused lessons explain German quality-origin categories, Alsace VT/SGN and bottle-ageing qualifications at an appropriate introductory level. Generic late-harvest wording is not used to claim that Alsace VT is necessarily dry or that late harvest universally requires noble rot.

All 20 new IDs and seven reused IDs were present in the current required-study catalog when checked, and all 67 new production IDs were represented. This was a static relationship check, not proof of semantic completeness or runtime behaviour. Root subsequently removed the two numeric OIV definition lessons from the required denominator and kept them optional; the generic old-vine explanation remains required.

Fog, cloud and mist need not be cloned into a generic lower-level lesson: actual Level 2-accessible named examples already teach these mechanisms. Examples include `ki_reg_am_russian_river_pinot`, `ki_reg_na_sta_rita_grapes`, `ki_reg_oa_hunter_moderation`, `ki_reg_sh_elgin_ripening` and `ki_wset_apply_sauternes_environment`. Their regional placement must remain visible in the catalog.

## Source and legal checks

- [OIV-VITI 703-2024](https://www.oiv.int/fr/node/3806): I identified a missing qualification in `ki_wset_close_oiv_old_vineyard`: the parcel must produce vitivinicultural products eligible for lawful certification by a competent authority. Root repaired that clause. The individual-vine graft-union distinction is retained and the recommendation is correctly distinguished from worldwide enforceable labelling permission. Both numeric definitions are now optional enrichment.
- [European Commission quality-scheme explanation](https://agriculture.ec.europa.eu/farming/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes/geographical-indications-and-quality-schemes-explained_en): wine-specific PDO and PGI grape-origin conditions are distinguished from the general food-production examples. The lessons do not turn PDO into a sensory-quality guarantee.
- [BC Wine Grape Council maturity guidance](https://bpg.bcwgc.org/harvest-quality/fruit-sampling-and-maturity/): sugar, aroma and phenolic maturity are treated separately. Seed browning and less readily extracted seed tannin are qualified; one sugar reading does not establish all maturity dimensions.
- [Winemakers Research Exchange cap-management overview](https://winemakersresearchexchange.com/library/fermentation/cap-management-punch-downs-and-pump-overs/): the pump-over and punch-down descriptions correctly distinguish operations and avoid an invariant gentleness ranking.
- [Iowa State vine structure/pruning guidance](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-prune-grapevines-home-garden): current green shoots, one-year canes and permanent trunk/cordon wood are differentiated without applying a home-garden pruning recipe to every commercial vineyard.
- [Alsace VT guidance](https://www.vinsalsace.com/fr/gouts-et-couleurs/aoc/vendanges-tardives/): the regional lesson preserves late concentration versus selective botrytised berries, rather than treating perfume or grape name as a sweetness measurement.
- [Seguin Moreau barrel/toast explanation](https://www.seguin-moreau.fr/en/wine-ranges/barrels-wine/): toast changes wood-derived aroma/tannin contributions; the lesson appropriately avoids equating heavier toast, smaller vessels or more expenditure with better wine.

## Regional grape roles and applications

The 27 grape-role texts distinguish contributions to local wines from grape permissions alone. Representative primary checks confirmed Dornfelder's regional fruit/wood range, Jaen/Mencía synonymy and Dão context, Argentine Bonarda's distinct identity, Friuli white/ramato and cellar examples, and the breadth of Castilla y León IGP. The [IVV Jaen page](https://www.ivv.gov.pt/casta/jaen/) was read through an actual HTTP fetch after the browsing renderer timed out; its displayed update is 19 February 2026. The [Castilla y León public specification](https://www.itacyl.es/documents/20143/0/PPCC%2BIGP%2BVTCYL%2B%282%29.pdf/dc9869da-3147-fa5a-273a-592a5e734187) is used for qualitative geographic context, not as an assertion that every 2011 numerical condition remains current.

I flagged all 18 new grape-role source jurisdiction fields, which had descriptive prose instead of country codes. The critic, with root authorization, repaired them to their actual country codes and preserved the relation dates. This finding is closed.

Root's European applications now include mechanisms rather than place-name substitutions: the Alentejo red trial explains extraction and its limit; white/red cases connect the supplied fruit with cellar choices; comparison costs specify labour, fruit rejection, bought wood or capacity/holding commitments. These are explicit supplied scenarios. They do not assert that every producer in that region follows the route, that a soil implies a flavour, or that higher costs prove better sensory quality or a specific retail price. Previous irrelevant Recioto/Cerasuolo citations in the northern-white/Abruzzo-red costs were repaired. The Bordeaux 2023 proposal is honestly classified as a reference for qualitative geographic context, not current legislation.
