# Diploma causal-reasoning starter: sources and limits

Authored on 27 September 2026. This is a small, original Q6 starter, not complete Diploma written-analysis practice or official assessment equivalence. It represents four conditional mechanisms as actual cited graph paths. The same broad mechanisms are already discussed in the principles curriculum; the new contribution is the ordered causal representation and explicit evidence against three proposed conclusions per exercise. No examination question, proprietary grid, plot or worked official example is reproduced.

## Inventory and editorial mapping

The area contains **25 nodes, 21 relations, 21 knowledge items, 21 aliases, 21 mappings, two new sources and 22 citations**. Five primary references are used: two new and three canonical existing records. Four nodes contain full hypothetical premises, five represent intermediate states, four the correct conclusions and twelve the proposed wrong conclusions. There are no geography nodes or containment/location edges.

The template file contains four forward reasoning exercises and six forward recall templates, one flashcard and one typed template for each new relation. The positive paths have lengths **2, 3, 2 and 2**. `CAUSES_STATE` connects a premise to a state; `LEADS_TO` connects states. Neither relation is declared transitive. All twelve negative facts use a current, cited `CONTRADICTS` edge directly from the relevant premise to its proposed wrong conclusion. No answer is excluded merely because it is missing from the graph.

Every point is `unverified` and has `mcq_disabled: true`: generic relation distractors cannot establish these conditional alternatives. The separate reasoning templates explicitly identify their three contradictory options and evidence. Every timestamp is a quoted UTC string with whole milliseconds. `valid_from` records curation, not a legal effective date.

All mappings are **WSET_L4 only**. The four final target items are core at depth 4; the five supporting edges are core at depth 3; the twelve contradiction facts are secondary at depth 2. Evidence and explanation for a wrong conclusion serve feedback, not a second assessed answer. Generic term recall is available for each relation, but it does not establish essay-writing competence or expert verification. The official syllabus establishes the study scope and is not cited as proof of a physical or chemical mechanism.

## Conditional conclusions and contradiction QA

- **Water:** observed closure → reduced stomatal CO₂ entry → reduced assimilation through that limitation. Proposed increased CO₂ entry, increased assimilation through closure and increased cooling capacity through the closing stomata each contradict the stated physiological route. Controlled leaf temperature does not imply increased transpiration.
- **Bloom:** active basal-leaf removal without compensation → lower inflorescence carbohydrate supply → lower fruit set → fewer berries per cluster. Increased supply, increased set through deprivation and whole-cluster removal contradict different parts of the supplied conditions. The last contrast distinguishes leaf removal from cluster thinning.
- **MLF:** successful isolated conversion → malic replaced by lactic acid → lower TA from that replacement. Increased malate, absent lactate formation and an increased acid contribution contradict the conversion. None is justified by unrelated whole-wine processes excluded from the premise.
- **SO₂:** higher pH at equal free SO₂/alcohol/temperature → lower molecular SO₂ → weaker protection from that antimicrobial contribution. Greater molecular concentration, a pH-independent fraction and a lower free-SO₂ requirement at higher pH contradict the primary relationship. A real wine's final stability still depends on additional microbial barriers.

The premises and controlled comparisons are editorial hypotheses, not reported experiment observations. Citations support each mechanism and each contradicted conclusion; they do not turn the hypotheses into a claim that a source measured the scenario. Four targets and their explicit alternatives are a starter for objective causal practice, while broader written analysis and qualified expert review remain outstanding.
